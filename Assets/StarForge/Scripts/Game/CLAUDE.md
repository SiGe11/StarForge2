# Benchmark, evaluation, adaptive resolution — area notes

Root `CLAUDE.md` has the commands and the short measuring checklist; this holds the reasoning and the baselines. Read it before interpreting any fps number or touching a quality preset.

## Measuring frame rate
- `-sfplay` keeps vsync and the 60 cap, as a match runs — the frame rate a player sees, and the one to compare builds on. Without it the run is uncapped with vsync off, which on 6000.6.1 comes out bimodal (~9/60 ms). Capped frames quantise to whole refreshes (16.7/33.3 ms). Compare by the average, against a build measured the same way; `-sfcap60` matches builds from before the uncapping fix. Mechs land at ~4 minutes: use 420 s.
- **Record `pmset -g batt` with every number**: runs while the battery charged came out ~3 fps lower; on battery the Mac turns Low Power Mode on, and a long evaluation drains it even on the charger.
- **A locked screen caps the player at 20 fps** (every frame 50 ms; two runs came out at exactly 20.0 when the keep-awake ended and the Mac locked). Check `ioreg -n Root -d1 -a | plutil -extract IOConsoleUsers json -o - -` for `CGSSessionScreenIsLocked` before believing a slow run; unlocking needs the user.
- The Neo is fanless and drifts ~1 ms as it heats: compare rendering changes in alternating pairs against the previous build.
- The report's "simulation ..." and "frames over 40 ms ..." lines (`GameWorld.SimMs/TickedMs`, `GameBootstrap.MechBrainMs`, GCs, NavMesh island builds) say whether a long frame had a slow simulation step. The `frame timing:` line (`FrameTimingManager`; `BuildMac` sets `enableFrameTimingStats`) gives main/render thread and present-wait; **its GPU time is not usable on Metal** (frames overlap: GPU times exceed the frame).
- Mech fights are bound by cost that does not shrink with resolution; measure shadow distance and grass with `-sfshadows N` / `-sfdensity X`.
- Flicker/shimmer: measure on the built player (command in root file). Editor captures did not reproduce shimmer the player showed.
- Gallery: use the player's `-sfgallery`, not editor captures (the Editor's Game view is ~810x375). Takes the same shots every run on the default map (base close/mid, ore, shore, a grove before/during/after fire, animal, songbird flock feeding then flushed, overview, first fire-fight, the Mechs `9_mech_close`/`9b_mech_mid`/`9c_mech_other`/`9d_mech_fight_*`/`9e_mech_bay`).
- `-sfscreentest` (no `-sfbench`) switches full screen and window four times, logs the sizes the player reports and quits; pass `-logFile` to read them (the terminal has no screen-recording permission, so that log is the check).
- Testing cheat (README): `Ctrl`/`Cmd`+`Shift`+`M`, +5000 ore, marks `GameWorld.cheated`. In play every match draws a new seed (the AI's per-match personality); benchmark and evaluation use `MatchSettings.fixedSeed`.

## Adaptive resolution (`View/AdaptiveResolution.cs`)
Every preset aims at one 60 Hz refresh (`targetMs` 16.9). Under vsync a frame shows for 16.7 or 33.3 ms, so load is judged by the share of frames that miss a refresh (`SyncedUpdate`: down past `missHigh`, up after a clean spell under `missLow`; High 0.28/0.10 ≈ 47 fps, the others 0.15/0.04 ≈ 52). The old length rule could never see room to climb (a vsynced frame never measures under 16.7 ms) and one heavy fight left a match at its floor. *High* once aimed at 21.5 ms ("~46 fps", 160 m shadows, floor 0.80): 47.6 uncapped but 39 in play.

## Baselines (`-sfplay` 420 s, plugged in, battery not charging)
*High* (150 m shadows, full grass, floor 0.55, `missHigh` 0.28): **49.3 fps, 47.3 with a Mech in view**, render scale 0.62 (two runs within 0.2); with a 0.60 floor before Mechs were fitted out at the drop: 49.8 / 50.1. The armoury's rotary cannons and missile racks are the busiest effects (missile trails puff every 1.5 m for ~1 s; the rotary cannon's hits show every sixth round). With a Mech in view: 160 m shadows 45.3, a 0.70 floor 42.6; 160 m/1.0 grass 43.3, 110/1.0 46.1, 110/0.7 49.2 (all at the 0.60 floor).
Evaluation: `StarForge/Evaluate AI/Quick`/`Full` caps 720 s / 900 s; line per match carries both sides' Mech Bay and Mech and how the AI's Mech spent its time by intent. `AIEvalMenu.Replay` (`ReplayKind`/`ReplayGame`, via `editor.sh call`) logs both Mechs' reasoning every 30 s but **a replay does not repeat the match exactly** (frame timing differs): read it for behaviour, not result. A quick evaluation straight after a benchmark/gallery run crawled (18 s of play in ten minutes) because the player had taken the foreground: see App Nap in the root file.
