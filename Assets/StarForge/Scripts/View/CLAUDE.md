## Blast pressure (`SF_Wind.hlsl`, shared by SF_Tree and SF_Grass)
A muzzle blast or burst queues a front in `FXDirector.PressureWave` (expands at 52 m/s, spends itself as it spreads). `UploadGusts` puts the four strongest into `_SF_Gusts` (xy origin, z front radius now, w shove in metres) and `_SF_GustShape` (xy heading, z how much it favours it, w thickness); `_SF_GustCount` is 0 the rest of the time so the loop costs a compare. A plant reads fronts from its own root position (no per-plant state; a seed replays identically). The shove goes with the square of a blade's height and a tree vertex's distance from the trunk's foot (roots stay planted); both shaders renormalise the displaced vertex to its old distance from the root (a hard push bends, not stretches). **Clear the globals when the match stops**, or the field stays bent on the end screen.
**The pressure draws nothing of its own** (no drawn ring — `SF_GroundDecal` kind 5 is retired — and no screen-space refraction in the fog pass). It *moves things* as it arrives: `FXDirector.PressureSweep` follows each front's annulus (`Gust.swept`) and ruffles water (small dark rings — a roughened surface reflects less sky — a few faint bright ones and spray; the wake's white rings made the lake look like snow), lifts dust off dry ground with clods close in, tears leaves from crowns it crosses, jolts the camera when it reaches `RTSCamera.Focus`. Anything new it should move goes there; anything that would only *show* the front does not. `AgentPlay.PressureLook` holds a front still at several strengths with FXDirector off, each against the next frame with it off — the only way to see what the shove alone did (`Temp/pressure_*.png`).

## Smoke is the thing most easily got wrong
- `FX_Smoke` runs at `_Density` 1.4, `_SoftFade` 1.0 (set in `SceneAssembler`): the atlas feathers so wide that at 1.0 every plume was too faint on sunlit ground.
- The soft fade blends a puff out where geometry sits just behind it, so **smoke born on geometry is invisible** (the Mauler's exhaust, born on its engine deck, was unseen until moved 0.5 m above the stacks). `AgentPlay.MaulerShowReport` counts particles above the deck. Measure before re-tuning something you cannot see.
- Atlas `Art/Textures/smoke_puffs.png` (normal, occlusion, coverage; `python3 Tools/make_smoke_puffs.py`): coverage **never reaches opaque**, outline eroded at two scales; `SF_Smoke` must not rescale coverage back to 1 (it once divided by 0.6) or a puff draws as a solid sprite and a burst as a hard dark lump. Billows are warped ellipses over a domain-warped field, not hemispheres (the first version's were countable; its value noise showed a star in every puff).
- Lit by sun and from above, picked and eroded per particle through custom vertex streams (`FXDirector.SmokeStreams`: stable random, age). With the sun behind the camera every billow faces it and the cloud goes flat; the light from above keeps the volume. Particle colours are grey 0.3-0.5, not near-black (a 0.17 grey came out as a hole in the scene). Fire and glows still use photographed sheets (`SF_Particle`) / flipbooks (`SF_Flipbook`).
- When cutting smoke back, cut what comes off moving units (exhaust, track dust, shell trails), **not** what an explosion or fire makes: that is where the drama lives.

## Fire and explosion flipbooks, blast physics
- Fire and explosions are simulated flipbooks (`SF_Flipbook`; `fx_explosion.png` 3072 = 8x8 x 384, `fx_flame.png` 2048 = 8x8 x 256, from `Tools/blender/render_flipbooks.py`), used only while `FX_Fire`/`FX_Flame` are on `StarForge/Flipbook` (`SceneAssembler.RebuildFxMaterials`), else the old sheets. `blast` plays the whole fireball-to-smoke sheet per explosion; `fire` only its fireball stretch, drawn 2.4x; a flame tongue a random quarter of the campfire.
- **Importer: `fx_*` are scaled up to a power of two** (`npotScale` ToLarger, max 4096): kept at 3072 the sheet is never block-compressed (RGBA32, 50 MB with mips, even with BC7 set by name); at 4096 it is BC7, 22 MB.
- **Premultiplied, so draw order matters** (additive fire never cared): `blast` and `fire` sort by distance within the system and carry `sortingFudge` -10/-5 against the smoke; `flames` (1400) is left unsorted for cost (tongues of one fire overlap in any order without showing it).
- **Every `Emit(fire, …)` is made upright** (`upright` in `Emit`): spin folded into ±6° and lifted 0.45 x size, since the cell is an upright column with the fireball low in it; callers keep passing 0-360. A fireball near the ground reads as a small explosion: a bush catching or burning flares in tongues (`PlantIgnited`, the rolling billow is trees only). Crown billows 0.5/s at 1.3-2.0 x size, firelight glows dim (0.43/0.19/0.05) and wide (6-9 x size): at 1.5/s, 2-3 x and full colour every burning crown wore a round fireball and orbs hung over the fire (gallery `5b_fire`, before/after on the fixed seed).
- The explosion stands on the ground at `BlastGround` (0.13 of the cell, `Tools/blender/flipbooks/fx_explosion.json`): emit it upright, centred `(0.5 - BlastGround)` of its size above the burst's foot. Its own smoke replaces most billows (1, a building 3). A blended 8x8 sheet played whole stops at 63/64, or its last frame crossfades into the flash at cell 0.
- Fire and smoke colour are set per material by `SceneAssembler.Flipbook` (`_FireMid` 1/0.42/0.08, `_FireHot` 1/0.72/0.32, gain 2.4 explosion / 3.5 flame, `_SmokeColor` 0.29/0.27/0.25; re-run with menu Build/4b Rebuild FX Materials): ACES turns a pale hot colour at high gain into cream.
- `EmitFlame` takes sizes in the old sheet's units (a tongue half its quad) and draws 0.6 wide x 0.8 tall, foot kept: callers stay as they were.
- **Blasts shove what lies loose, presentation only:** `UnitWreck.Blast` (wakes a settled wreck; every shove restarts its freeze timer and puts off its sinking (3 s, a wake 4 s), so nothing freezes or sinks in the air, but never past `lieUntil`, its own time + 6 s: a shove more than 2 s past its time is ignored, or a fight beside it would keep it awake for ever; shove weighted rifleman 1, Skimmer 0.45, Digger 0.3, Mauler 0.12) and `DebrisBurst.Blast` (a burst too near its end to land again is left alone). Hard terrain landings reach `FXDirector.Landed` via `ContactRelay` on each body (PhysX sends collisions only to the body's own GameObject). Sparks and Mech casings use particle world collision at Low quality.
- `MechBannerCloth` (made by `MechView.Build`): the Mech's standard is a Unity `Cloth` pinned to a crossbar at the model's `Banner` pivot, a sibling of the rigid banner, which it hides while it runs; the Mech's own motion (`worldVelocityScale`/`worldAccelerationScale`) and `Wind.At` move it. **The rigid banner and its 20 ms spring (`MechView.Banner`) show instead whenever the cloth cannot run**: during the drop (it is made inactive and first switched on by the first `Tick` after landing, so the fall and the landing jolt are never simulated), above 3.5x, through a frame over 0.1 s (a hitch), after `MaxResets` 3 rebuilds, and on a wreck. `Resume` clears its motion when a Mech comes back out of fog. Once a second it reads the free corner back (`Cloth.vertices` allocates) and rebuilds if it is NaN/Inf or over 3 m from the crossbar (it rests 1.16 away, swings to ~1.45, can reach ~2.5 at most: only a bad cloth passes 3). The Foundries carry no banner. `-sfnocloth` on the player keeps the rigid one (fps A/B). Check: `BannerClothCheck.Report` (play mode): tip 1.08-1.44, 0 resets, cloth root on and rigid off at 1x; the reverse at 8x; a rear render from `Unity_RunCommand` shows the cloth (mesh, so a camera render catches it).
- **`InfantryCape`** (made and ticked by `UnitView`, soldiers only: `Eligible` -- infantry class, not a harvester): at each promotion a soldier without one rolls `Earned(unit id, new rank)` < 0.81 (a hash, never the match's stream: 81.0% over 200,000 ids); a further stripe recolours its trim. **It follows the unit's visibility itself** (`Tick(shown, ...)`: UnitView's renderer list is taken at `Bind`, before any cape exists, so without it an enemy cape stood in the fog). Shaped in body space (the model faces +Z): shoulders at y 1.50, z -0.20 under the pauldrons, under the pack to y 1.02, then out from under it and nearly straight to the hem at 0.22, half-width 0.52 to 0.78 (the user's references: a cloak, a cope, a papal cape, a space marine's). **Folds are broad and smooth** (`Fold`: two cosines, phase by unit id, at least six columns to a fold): alternating columns in and out made a sawtooth hem the user rejected. **The outer columns and the last row are an embroidered trim in the rank's colour**, the lower corners a palmette: a grid point on a trim seam gets a vertex per part round it, so colour and texture change sharply there, and **Unity Cloth welds coincident vertices** (255 render vertices, 210 particles; 182 before), so `cloth.vertices`/`coefficients` are in particle order, not the mesh's, and in no documented order: read a particle by what it is (the give-up check takes the lowest at rest), never by index (`RestParticles` for trials). The trims' UVs run the fabric's band (v 0.027-0.209, rope to rope) along each edge at its own proportions, mirrored in U: `SFAssetPostprocessor` sets wrap U Mirror for `cape_fabric` (its blanket Repeat for `Art/Textures` overrides the importer API). Vertex colours made `.linear` (raw they came out pale), the folds left to the light; the banner's Particles Simple Lit material (no new shader) with `FXDirector.capeFabric` (`Art/Textures/cape_fabric.png`, set by `SceneAssembler`: a grey weave above, the embroidered band at the bottom) as its base map, brightened x6.4 (1 / the weave's linear average, 0.156). **`Material.SetColor` takes gamma values and converts them in this linear project: pass a linear gain as `new Color(g, g, g).gamma`** -- given 6.4 raw it applied ~59 and the capes came out pale cyan with white hems. Cloth only while near the camera's focus (42 m on, 50 off), at most 24 at once (nearest first, **one already cloth ranked 6 m nearer** so the 24th and 25th do not trade places as the camera pans), at or below 3.5x, and **not for a second after a frame over 0.1 s** (a long step blows a cloth up; switched back the next frame, every cape flicked on each hitch). Otherwise `cloth.enabled` false draws the rest mesh, leant back on a spring **about the line where it leaves the pack** (y 1.02; leant about the shoulders the stretch under the pack went back out through it; `Euler(+lean)` takes the hem back). The held rows have `maxDistance` 0, the skirt up to 0.55 with the backstop at its rest surface (normals face out behind him), **less toward the edges** (`EdgeHold` 0.5, linear in |x| over the half-width): the backstop stops a particle only along its normal, and sideways a hem corner slid ~0.5 m in front of a leg (143-380 particle-frames inside a leg per ~1 M a type, 0.15 deep). At death it rides the body into the wreck, rigid, and **bunches up toward the shoulders** over 0.6 s (scale 0.55/0.24/0.4): left hung and flared it stood off a body on its side or face down like a sail (`Temp/cape_death_*.png`). `LocalBounds` reads MeshFilters only, so the wreck's box is unchanged; `OnDestroy` destroys its mesh; the statics reset at every play entry (`ResetStatics`, SubsystemRegistration); `Worn` counts capes on the living (`Live` includes wrecks'); the sort uses a cached `IComparer` (a `Comparison` allocates on Mono). `-sfnocapes` on the player turns them off and `-sfallcapes` caps every soldier from training (the budget full: the fps A/B; the benchmark report's `capes:` line). ## Ragdolls, ground strikes, weather
- **A rifleman dies as a ragdoll** (`UnitWreck.MakeLegs`/`Hips`): `LegL`/`LegR` (pivots at the hips) become rigid bodies on `CharacterJoint`s to the torso (twist about the torso's right, given in the leg's own frame since an imported child may carry a turn: -25 back to +95 forward; swing 35 splay, 12 turn), 12/4 solver iterations, collisions between neighbouring limbs ignored; the topple velocities go to the torso and each leg takes the torso's point velocity. Freeze, wake, shoves, water drag and the "still" test run over every body (`bodies`). **A frozen body's colliders are off and their bounds collapse to the transform**: measure limbs from their renderers (GroundLook read 0 degrees on every hip that way). **Joint sign: in Unity's convention positive twist about right folds the leg forward** (the Ragdoll Wizard's knees are -80..0, hips -20..70); the first version had it mirrored. Check: `GroundLook.Run`/`Report` (signed hip flex mid-fall and at rest -21..+106 degrees, leg-to-hip gap, lowest point and top, which way the body faces, a footfall kick re-woken; read the rest pose before 7 s: a body sinks after 7-9).
- **Ground under a strike** (`FXDirector.Ground`): `GroundAt` blends dust/clod colours and amounts by `MapGenerator.SplatAt` (meadow, gravel, rock, sand), ash on burnt grass (`Vegetation.GrassState` 2), mud within 0.8 m above the water. A Mech's dry footfall is `GroundStrike` (dust, clods, footprint); `OnFootfall` then kicks wrecks and chunks within 2.8 m (`UnitWreck.Blast`/`DebrisBurst.Blast`). Use it for anything new that strikes the ground.
- **Weather** (`FXDirector.Weather`, view only, drawn from the map seed by `Hash`, 30% rain days; seed 1000 clear; `-sfweather rain|clear`; `ForceRain`/`ForceShower` statics for checks, cleared by whoever sets them, then `RedrawWeather`): showers from Perlin noise on the match clock (thresholds 0.54-0.66: 11-25% of a rain day's first minutes on the seeds read; at 0.42-0.62 it rained more than half the time); wetness rises at 0.05/s and dries at 0.011/s into the global `_SF_Rain` (SF_Terrain, SF_Unit, SF_Grass, SF_Rock); Atmosphere fog/haze/scatter and the sun's intensity follow the shower and are put back by `ClearWeather` when the director is disabled (scene reload, play stop). `GameWorld.running` never goes false at a match's end, so a shower carries on behind the end screen. Rain emission is capped by the system's free room (8000) and a 0.1 s step (8x frames). **Rain rings only within 30 m of the camera's focus**: further out a ring is a few pixels and the lake turned to a speckle; beyond, `SF_Water` reads `_SF_Shower` (the shower now; `_SF_Rain` is ground wetness, which lags and outlasts it) to damp the glint and dull the surface. Rings leave the ripple pool 100 of its 400. Gust dust sheets need wind over 0.55 and dry, loose ground (`dustAmount` 0.8); dust devils on days over 0.55, 35-80 s apart, die over meadow, wet ground or water.

## Unit deaths (`UnitWreck`)
On the frame a mobile unit dies `UnitView` hands its `body` (and the Mauler's turret / the Trooper's `Gun`, its legs: jointed limbs, see Ragdolls above) to a new wreck object and does nothing more; `GameWorld` still destroys the unit after 1.3 s and the simulation is unchanged. The wreck is rigid bodies on the terrain collider (follows craters): a box from the mesh bounds (readable when mesh data is not), centre of mass low, raised out of the ground if the NavMesh had it buried, frozen kinematic once still (or at 7 s) and sunk. It falls the way `Unit.killDir` points, as hard as `killForce` (set by `GameWorld.Kill`: away from a shell's burst when `Damage(..., splash)`, else from the shooter; fallback direction from the unit id, never the match's random stream). A Trooper's topple is given about his feet (linear velocity `fall x spin x hips`): spun about his middle his feet dug in and he stopped at 25 degrees. Machines char through `_Damage` (0.9 falling to 0.6 so glowing cracks go out) with a low `_Burn` (0.32 to 0): at the debris' full `_Burn` a hull glowed like lava and bloomed to a yellow lump. `FXDirector.Wrecks` burns/smokes by `Heat` and dusts a skidding Skimmer; `TrooperDown` replaces the fireball a rifleman used to die in (AudioDirector and GroundMask skip the boom and scorch for him). Only what the player saw die leaves a wreck; past 36 the oldest sink early. `AgentPlay.DeathLook` photographs one of each (`Temp/death_*.png`, 2x).

## Mauler weight
`FXDirector.MaulerEffects` (engine smoke off the two stacks, dust and stones off the tracks, barrel smoke after a shot) + the hull's recoil rock in `UnitView` (`RecoilRock`, damped swing; the turret takes its yaw as a *local* rotation so it rides the hull). Stack and track positions are the model's (`Tools/blender/build_models.py`: stacks 0.62 m out on the engine deck, track runs 1.30 m out): moving them in Blender means moving them here. Engine smoke goes through the `trail` system, not `smoke` (a base on fire fills `smoke` itself, and a burning structure is the cue the player needs at a distance); the rate thins with camera distance (`RTSCamera.distance`). Tuning landed on the third round (cloud the size of the tank → unseen haze → a wisp at idle and a grey-black plume under load). The ground keeps the trail: tread marks last 60 s, `GroundMask` stamps channel A (a crater's thrown soil) along a Mauler's path, healing over minutes. Sound: `AudioDirector.TankVoice` — the three loudest Maulers in earshot get an engine and a track source each, engine pitch/level following load, clatter following speed (`AgentPlay.TankVoices`). `AgentPlay.MaulerShow`/`MaulerShowReport` stage one in a grove (idling, driving, firing, shell landing; `Temp/mauler_*.png`); `AgentPlay.SmokeLook` photographs a shell burst, a structure going up, a tree burning (`Temp/smoke_*.png`).

## Fauna (`Fauna.cs`)
Quaternius FBX in `Art/Fauna/`, imported Legacy-animated with no materials; `SceneAssembler.BuildFauna` colours the parts, turns each model to face +Z (rig bones are unnamed, so each facing is given), sizes it from its skeleton (imported renderer bounds are metres off), saves `Prefabs/Fauna_*`. Ground animals step only on NavMesh for ground units; flyers keep level over the highest ground under their circle. Songbirds (`perches`) feed on the ground (hop, peck, look), flush as a flock, fly a bounding flight to another open patch and land: they need `fold`/`peck` clips and open spots (`Vegetation.UnderCrown`). Animals are drawn about twice life size or they vanish into the grass at this camera. View-only and fog-fair: shown and frightened only by what the player could see. `-sfnofauna` omits them (~0.3 ms).

## Audio (`AudioDirector`)
- `AudioDirector.bank` (a `SoundBank` SceneAssembler fills from `Audio/`) with synth fallbacks. `PlayAt` takes a free voice or the one nearest its end (`FreeVoice`), never simply the next in turn; idle tank loops are paused.
- **Agent runs play muted** (root `CLAUDE.md`; `Editor/AgentAudio.cs` sets the Game view's Mute Audio). It changes nothing the checks read: `RifleCheck` muted, with sound, muted again: every shot on a real voice, 0 virtualised, 15/14/15 voices at the busiest (the shot count follows the fight: 30/18/28); the player's test mute (`AudioListener.volume` 0, `Game/TestAudio.cs`) likewise: 0 virtualised, 16 real voices against 15 at volume 1.
- **The mining take plays for a Digger at its ore and mining** (`Unit.working`, as FXDirector's dust): keyed on an empty load, a Digger topping up a part load mined in silence (`BugTrials.MineSound`: a take playing in 1% of frames with part loads, 83% now; empty loads 67-76%).
- **Loudness is relative**: "the rifle has no sound" was a shot at 0.15 under music at 0.54, not a missing clip. `AgentPlay.RifleCheck`/`RifleCheckReport` stage a fire-fight and report every shot's source (started, audible, virtualised) before anything is changed. `AgentPlay.AudioReport` prints what the bank actually loaded (the only check that `make_audio.py`'s output is wired in).
- Music: `music_<calm|tension|combat>_<n>` tracks, one mood at a time on two crossfading sources, each at `musicLoudness` / its RMS from `music.json`; the benchmark report prints how much each mood played. The calm set (three, played in turn) must be warm: a minor-key piano loop under a quiet base reads as creepy (see `Tools/CLAUDE.md` for the measuring tool and thresholds).

## HUD (`UI/HUDController`, UI Toolkit)
**Nothing the HUD runs every frame builds elements**: a row is rebuilt only when what it shows changes (the selection panel's queue row compares the selected unit, its research and its queue item by item -- a hashed key could collide once a unit both researched and trained) and its bars are moved in place between; rebuilt every frame, a Foundry with five queued made ~5.8 KB of garbage a frame and new elements to lay out. Check `BugTrials.HudAlloc` (the HUD's `Update` alone, Mono heap across the call, which steps in 4 KB blocks: read a few hundred bytes as noise): 0-341 B a call for nothing, a Digger, a Foundry idle or with five queued, a Mech Bay researching (five queued: 5,803 before); the queue row 5 slots for 5, its bar moving, 4 slots once one comes out; the research row one row, its bar moving, gone when done. `PlayerController` builds the command card for its hotkeys only on a frame a key went down (built every frame to look, 225-532 B a frame; `BugTrials.CardAlloc`: 205 B a call now, the rest of its Update).


## Colour and glow
- Glow is emission read by bloom (threshold 1, clamped at 24). Ore uses `SF_Unit`'s `_Crystal` path, reading per-shard data from vertex colour G/B (`sf_model.set_vdata`); the Digger's `ore_glow` material follows `_OreGlow`, set per renderer by `UnitView`. The crystal *emission*, not the albedo, carries the colour (glossy facets reflecting the pale sky washed it to white).
- **Ore colour lives in two places that must agree:** `SFMaterialLibrary` (crystal and ore_glow emission, violet rim) and `FXDirector.OreLight/OreRim`; the ground pool's breath (`FXDirector.OreBreath`) uses the crystal shader's phase and period.
- WASD pans the camera (unless a modifier is held): command hotkeys must not use W, A, S or D.


## Kept results (`FogOfWarRenderer`, `VegetationRenderer`)
- `FogOfWarRenderer` keeps its 128x128 fog targets and resamples only the texels round a grid cell that changed, when `GameWorld.VisVersion` moves (resampling every texel twice a frame cost ~35x the easing); the easing runs every frame, the upload only when a byte changed. Targets must stay bit-identical to a full resample.
- `VegetationRenderer` keeps each plant's pose until a `Vegetation.Live` field that `Vegetation.Pose` reads changes (`PoseKey`), and re-inits when the plant arrays are replaced. **A new changing input to `Pose` must join `PoseKey`**, or that plant keeps drawing its old pose.
- Check: `MathChecks.Run`/`Report` (play mode, ~50 s): fog targets against the old full resample, kept poses against `Pose` while trees fall, burn and are cratered, `NearestDropoff` against a full scan, then an in-place regeneration with fires burning. Target 0 mismatches and 0 exceptions (measured: 1,159 fog frames, 1.45 M plant-frames, 35 k Digger-frames, 841 → 940 plants with 44 burning).
