# Tools: asset fetching, packers, audio — area notes

Licence rules (root `CLAUDE.md`) apply to every fetch/pack here: verify → register in `THIRD-PARTY.md` + README credit → `python3 Tools/check_licences.py`.

## Sources and packers
CC0 only (public repo). `python3 Tools/fetch_assets.py` fetches into `Art/Source/` (git-ignored): Poly Haven ground, rock and plant scans (plants as .blend, ~700 MB: pass asset ids to fetch only some, e.g. `island_tree_01`), ambientCG leaf atlases, Kenney sound packs, Quaternius animated animals, KayKit's character animations (Rig_Medium and its clips, for `blender/build_soldier_rig.py`), OpenGameArt music, the recorded gunshots/tree falls/wood breaks (Free Firearm Sound Library, kheetor, AntumDeluge, rubberduck), JangaFX EmberGen VDBs (`--jangafx`). No 7z in the standard library: macOS `tar -xf` (libarchive) reads the firearm library's .7z. `afconvert -f WAVE -d LEI16@32000 -c 1 in out.wav` decodes ogg/mp3/wav for numpy work. Keep renders and staged files in the repo tree, not the scratchpad (a restart once wiped it).
Poly Pizza models are mixed licences (many CC-BY): check each. Poly Haven trees are millions of triangles, unusable directly (hence the bake).
Packers → committed assets: `blender/pack_textures.py` (terrain colour + scanned height in alpha, normals), `blender/build_rocks.py` (decimated rock FBXs + `rocks.json`, merged into `ModelFactory.Meta`), `blender/make_leaf_cards.py` (leaf-spray textures from ambientCG atlases), `pack_fauna.py`, `blender/build_songbird.py` (re-poses/re-animates the perched bird into a flying one with a palette texture; `TUCK` shuts the wings in the standing, pecking and folded poses: read a change from a Blender render first, `-- <preview.png> <out dir>` writes a trial copy outside the project), `blender/render_flipbooks.py` (EmberGen VDBs → flipbooks via Cycles; `blender/flipbooks/*.json`), `make_audio.py`, `blender/encode_audio.py` (Vorbis; macOS has no ogg encoder).
`pack_marks.py` (`/usr/bin/python3`: numpy and Pillow) packs six pictures the author made with an image model (`Art/Source/llm/`, git-ignored, not fetchable again) into `scorch.jpg` (2 x 2 blast marks, sRGB-encoded soot), `armor.png`'s alpha (the hulls' damage mask) and `fog_unknown.png` (the survey grid's lines); `--out DIR` stages them. Generated textures: `make_leaf_textures.py` (inner-canopy leaf pile), `make_flame_sheet.py`, `make_smoke_puffs.py`, `make_panel_texture.py` (hull plating `armor.png`/`armor_n.png` for SF_Unit; **it writes no alpha, and `armor.png`'s alpha is the damage mask: run `pack_marks.py` after it, or every hurt hull chars all over**; `_n` is its height, turned into normals on import). The water keeps the original's `water_n.jpg` ripples (a synthesised replacement was rejected: "waves were bad"; its foam, glint and reflection changes stay). Blender is the image library for scripts that need one (system Python has only numpy).
**Compile without the Editor:** `python3 Tools/offline_compile.py --use <Assets path>=<staged file> --add ... --patch <script>` builds Assembly-CSharp and -Editor with staged changes swapped in, using the Editor's own Roslyn and the response files of its last build; Assets/ is never touched. Check a batch with it before a write window, so the one compile in the shared Editor does not fail under a peer.
**numpy is in `/usr/bin/python3`**, not the Homebrew `python3` a plain call now finds: `analyse_burst.py` and `diff_shots.py` (two folders of PNGs, 8x box, worst block and worst pixel) run with `/usr/bin/python3` or Blender's own Python.
App icon: `Art/AppIcon.png` from `Assets/icon.png` by `python3 Tools/make_app_icon.py`; keep Apple's 824 px rounded-square template (macOS 26 puts any other shape on a grey plate).
**After a bake or pack that writes PNGs, run `/usr/bin/python3 Tools/optimize_pngs.py <paths>`** (dry run; `--stage DIR` then `--apply DIR` to write): lossless zlib recompression of the pixel data only, every other chunk kept (Unity reads `gAMA`), each file proved pixel-identical before it counts, 16-bit and no-gain files left alone. Blender and the packers write default-compression PNGs, which cost ~12% of the textures' 113 MB of LFS (the scanned-plant sheets 8-27%, `smoke_puffs` 61%); a bake hands that back. Not for Build-step outputs (`Art/Icons`, `Map/SF_*.png`: the next Build rewrites them). `--to-8bit` also converts a 16-bit RGB PNG (a scanned tree's bark) to 8-bit, rounded: lossy at the source (0.5/255), and Unity imports 8 bits anyway; the tool says so in its summary.

## Audio build (`make_audio.py`)
Copies Kenney effects and music under game names with `music.json` loudness, synthesises ambience loops, builds guns and wood (each shot mixed from a near and a far recording of the same gun with a slap-back, every take levelled to one loudness with `match()`). `--mech` builds only the Mech sounds, `--sidearms` only a soldier's (`/usr/bin/python3`: it has numpy): pistol centroid 1.0-1.2 kHz, knife 350-400 Hz, grenade 700-750 Hz with 3-16% under 120 Hz, flash bang 1.65 kHz.
- Weapons, blasts and engine beds are *built* from registered CC0 recordings: `build_weapon`/`build_blast` add a falling sub `thump()` and a `rolling()` report, then `hipass_lin(..., 45)` removes everything below 45 Hz. **The high-pass matters**: a 40 Hz sine carries enormous energy for its loudness, a laptop speaker cannot move it, and `match()` then turns the audible part down to make room (the first cannon was 95% below 120 Hz and came out a quiet thud).
- Nothing can be listened to from the agent's side. Check a new sound by the share of its energy under 120 Hz and its spectral centroid: rifle ~800 Hz, cannon ~180, engine bed ~220 (it was 83 Hz before its knock was soft-clipped and the bed high-passed), shell landing 240-320, structure 150-200.
- Music: judge a candidate with `python3 Tools/measure_music.py <file>` (key, share of time the harmony is minor, dissonant-interval/beating content, brightness, note density, swell; `--segments` catches a dark passage inside a warm track), not by its title. Aim: major key, minor_share well under 0.5, dissonance under ~0.05, swell under 3 (the calm set's first two tracks were 0.68 and 0.46 minor and sounded creepy). Tension and combat loop: prefer a seamless loop or a short tail (a finished piece's seconds of silence play every time round), and keep to the orchestral kind (chiptune and "mega man" pieces clash).
- **Add music with `/usr/bin/python3 Tools/make_audio.py --music [--out DIR]`**: incremental, a track already in `Audio/Music` keeps its file and `.meta` (the full run used to delete and rebuild all of them: new GUIDs). `--out Art/Source/staged/music` stages new tracks and a full `music.json` during a peer's play mode; copy them in after, then **Build step 4** (`SceneAssembler` fills the `SoundBank`; a file alone plays nothing; it also re-saves the tree materials and fauna prefabs, so announce it). Check: each new clip's `.meta` guid once in `Scenes/Battlefield.unity` and `rmsCalm`/`rmsTension`/`rmsCombat` there equal to `music.json` (a 0.1 is a missed entry); `AgentPlay.AudioReport` lists effects only. Numbers per mood must run on without a gap (the loader stops at the first missing one); `music.json` keeps `rms` first (the loader's regex). A WAV source is trimmed of edge silence (-50 dB of peak) and lifted to rms 0.06 if quieter: the game turns a track down, never up.
