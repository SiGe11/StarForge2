# The Annalist's briefing: the long intro script (not in use)

Written 2026-11-22 as a ten-minute intro film: a narration of about 1,130 words by an Annalist of
the Host, in ten chapters, with a shot list for each. The intro is now one ten-second clip
(`prompts.md`, prompt 6), because the video model makes clips of ten seconds at most. This file is
kept for the narration, which is the lore told in order in one voice, and in case a longer film is
ever cut together from short clips. Its shot prompts lean on a shared brief (3.1) and are not
written to be pasted one by one.

## The ten-minute version

### How to use this

Most video models make clips of 5 to 20 seconds, so this is written as one master brief plus ten
one-minute chapters, each with its narration and a shot list. Paste **3.1 and the whole of 3.3**
into a tool that accepts a long script; for a clip generator, paste **3.1 plus one shot at a
time**. Titles and the quoted line at the end are added in the edit: do not ask the model to
draw text. Tell me which tool you use and I will reshape the shot prompts to its limits.

Narrator: an Annalist of the Host (the archivists who keep a file on every officer), briefing the
player before a posting to Vesper. The Annalist never says which wing the listener serves.
Narration is about 1,130 words, about 115 words a minute with the pauses.

### 3.1 Master brief (paste first, every time)

> A ten-minute animated prologue for the strategy game StarForge. Style: animated painterly
> matte paintings, the look of high-end strategy-game key art brought to life with slow camera
> moves (push-ins, parallax, drifting cloud, falling ash, lamplight flicker). Not photorealistic,
> not cartoon, not anime. 16:9, 24 fps, 1080p or higher.
>
> World: science fiction with a medieval and liturgical flavour. Armoured soldiers in full
> helmets and ankle-length cloth capes; low heavy tanks; nine-metre walking war machines with a
> cloth standard on the back; plain stone halls open to the sky, lit by oil lamps, incense and
> hand bells; clergy in long white robes under stiff gold mantles.
>
> Colour script: the Bright Age is warm white-gold; the Quenching is gold draining to black; the
> Empire is cold slate and lamplight; the world Vesper is moss green, birch white and long
> twilight; the war is blue lights against orange-red lights with ember-orange fires.
>
> Recurring motifs, always drawn the same way: (1) the Brightest Star, one brilliant blue-white
> star with a faint four-point glint, visible at least once in every chapter; (2) teal ore:
> clusters of pale teal crystal shards with veins of light inside; (3) two identical armies, one
> marked blue and one orange-red; (4) the Holy Mother, veiled in white from crown to floor, face
> never seen; (5) the Makers, the ancient smiths, shown only as hands, backs and silhouettes.
>
> Never: readable text, letters, numbers, logos, subtitles, watermarks; modern Earth objects;
> glowing magic spells, wizards or energy beams from hands; gore; smiling to camera.
>
> Sound: a low male or female narrator, unhurried, dry, exact, a little tired; an archivist, not
> a herald. Music: unaccompanied chant in an invented language (no real liturgical text) and low
> strings; near silence for the Quenching; no drums until chapter 9.

### 3.3 Chapters

#### 1. Vespers in two camps (0:00-0:50)

Narration:

> At the first star, both camps sing. The same office, the same words, in a speech nobody has
> spoken for centuries. On a still evening each can hear the other. In the morning they will go
> back to killing one another, and nobody on this world can tell you by whose order.
> Before you take up your posting, Commander, the Annalists require that you hear how it came
> to this.

Shots:

- 1.1 (8 s) Black. One blue-white star appears and steadies. Silence, then a single chanting voice.
- 1.2 (8 s) Slow tilt down from the star to a moorland valley at dusk: spruce, lakes, pale teal crystals in dark rock.
- 1.3 (10 s) An army camp marked in blue. Soldiers kneel in rows in the iron (headless grey plate, a slit in the breastplate, blue shoulders, two masts on the back), a few in blue capes. A celebrant, a woman veiled to the waist, in white and gold, raises a lamp to the sky.
- 1.4 (8 s) Close: a thurible swings, incense rises past a parked tank's lowered gun.
- 1.5 (10 s) Across the valley, small and far, a camp marked in orange-red doing the same thing at the same moment. The chant arrives a heartbeat late, like an echo.
- 1.6 (6 s) Both camps in one wide frame, the star above. Hold.

#### 2. The Bright Age (0:50-1:55)

Narration:

> A thousand years ago our ancestors worked starfire. It was not sorcery. It was a craft, and
> its people were smiths. They held that it was sunlight brought down to the anvil.
> A thing made in that fire kept a purpose of its own. A wall that meant to stand. A plough that
> meant to cut straight. A machine that meant to guard.
> We call those smiths the Makers, because no name of theirs survives. Only the work.
> And every fire they ever lit was kindled from one hearth. The first. The Starforge.

Shots:

- 2.1 (10 s) A forge interior flooded with white-gold light that is plainly not ordinary flame: it looks like noon sun held in a hearth. Silhouettes of smiths at work.
- 2.2 (8 s) Close on hands only: tongs lift a glowing bar; the glow is white-gold, steady, without smoke.
- 2.3 (9 s) A city wall under construction at sunrise. A great stone is lowered and settles into place by itself with a small, deliberate shift.
- 2.4 (8 s) A field at dawn. A plough with no ox and no ploughman cuts a perfectly straight furrow; a farmer walks behind it with his hands in his belt.
- 2.5 (10 s) A tall guardian machine of bronze-dark metal stands at a gate. Its head turns slowly to follow a child running past. A warm light behind its faceplate.
- 2.6 (10 s) Runners carry covered lamps away from one small hilltop hearth, along roads, in every direction, like sparks leaving a fire.
- 2.7 (10 s) The hearth itself: small, plain, stone, on a bare hill, its fire needing no fuel. Slow push in.

#### 3. The Quenching (1:55-3:00)

Narration:

> Then, in a single night, it ended.
> Nine watches were kept over the Starforge that night. The fire failed during the ninth. Every
> starfire went out with it. The purposed things kept their purpose, and lost the power to act
> on it alone.
> Why? You will be given three answers. The clergy say the fire was lent by the stars, and has
> gone home. The Khan's chroniclers say it was spent, in a war nobody recorded. And the knightly
> orders say the Makers put it out themselves; that the orders swore something that night; and
> that they do not discuss it.
> The archive holds no fourth account. We named what followed the Long Night. We are living in
> it still.

Shots:

- 3.1 (8 s) Night. The hilltop hearth, nine armoured guards in a ring with their backs to us, a lamp at each one's feet. Music stops.
- 3.2 (8 s) Time passing: one by one eight of the lamps burn out. The ninth still burns.
- 3.3 (9 s) The hearth's white-gold light sinks, gutters, and is gone. The stone goes dark. Nobody moves.
- 3.4 (8 s) A city seen from far off: its lights go out district by district, then the next city along the coast, then the next.
- 3.5 (8 s) The guardian machine at the gate stops in mid-step. Its head lowers. The light behind its faceplate dims to a faint point and stays there.
- 3.6 (6 s) First answer: a white-robed figure on a roof at night points up at the star-filled sky.
- 3.7 (6 s) Second answer: a plain of ash and broken war machines under a red sky, no one alive, no banners.
- 3.8 (6 s) Third answer: hands in smiths' gloves lowering a stone cover over a small bright fire, a ring of armoured guards kneeling round it.
- 3.9 (6 s) Darkness. Then far overhead, alone, the blue-white star.

#### 4. The Compact and the two hands (3:00-4:10)

Narration:

> Three hundred years of war between the houses followed. The first Matriarch ended it. She
> chose one war-captain to stand over all the others, on a single condition: that he never rule
> the faith. That bargain is the Compact, and it has kept the peace for seven centuries.
> So the Empire has two hands, and they may never become one. The Khan holds: the Host, the
> courts, the treasury. The Holy Mother blesses, and at each Khan's death she alone elects the
> next.
> And the capital is neither of their seats. In law, the Empire is ruled from the Brightest Star
> itself: the one light that stands in the night sky of every world we hold. Nobody lives there.
> A new Khan sails within sight of it once, to be invested. He does not go again.

Shots:

- 4.1 (8 s) House wars: riders and foot under many different banners clash on a winter field, lamps and torches, no starfire anywhere.
- 4.2 (9 s) A ring of exhausted war-captains in a field tent. A woman veiled in white from crown to floor walks among them and lays her hand on one man's shoulder.
- 4.3 (7 s) Close: two different seals pressed side by side into wax on an unseen document.
- 4.4 (8 s) The Camp: a great stone city whose streets and halls are laid out like the rows of a war camp, banners over it, a saddle-shaped high seat in an open court.
- 4.5 (8 s) The Mother Hall: a long plain stone hall, a round opening in the roof above the altar, a pierced screen hung with lamps and gold star-icons.
- 4.6 (10 s) Nine nights: the veiled Matriarch keeps vigil alone under the opening in the roof; the sky wheels above her.
- 4.7 (12 s) A ship's bridge in silence. A man in a heavy fur-collared cloak stands at the glass. Before him, filling the view, a blue-white giant star. He kneels.
- 4.8 (8 s) The same star as a single point in the night sky above three different worlds in turn: a desert, a harbour, a snowfield.

#### 5. The light received, the Host in four wings (4:10-5:00)

Narration:

> The old sun-cult widened as we spread among the stars. Every sun is a star, every star a sun,
> all light one kindred. Since the fire went out, the light can no longer be worked. Only
> received.
> And the Empire's army, the Host, is kept in equal wings, so that no captain ever commands all
> of it. Two at the founding, Blue and Red. Four since a cautious Khan split them: Green and
> Yellow beside the old two. The same equipment. The same ranks. The same oath, sworn under the
> Brightest Star: by the hand that holds, and the hand that blesses.

Shots:

- 5.1 (9 s) The Great Liturgy at night: celebrant and people all facing the same way, toward the altar and the open sky; lamps, incense, kneeling.
- 5.2 (7 s) Close: a shallow silver dish of water standing on a parapet under the open night sky, stars reflected in it.
- 5.3 (8 s) A gloved hand dips a sprig in the dish and sprinkles a folded military standard.
- 5.4 (10 s) A parade ground at dusk seen from above: an army drawn up in four exactly matched quarters, marked blue, orange-red, green and yellow, empty avenues between them.
- 5.5 (8 s) Face-on: two soldiers, one of each colour, identical armour and rifles, right hands raised in the same oath.
- 5.6 (8 s) A soldier kneels; an ankle-length cape in his wing's colour, edged with embroidered trim, is laid on his shoulders and sprinkled with water.

#### 6. Vesper and the Seam Survey (5:00-6:00)

Narration:

> Now, Vesper. It is named for the hour: from that world, the Brightest Star is the first light
> of evening.
> A quiet world. Birch and spruce, wolf and eagle, and for centuries, smallholders. A hundred
> years ago the Muster cleared them off the land for ore concessions. The concessions failed.
> The farms went back to birch and wolves.
> What grows in Vesper's rock is quenchstone. The soldiers call it teal. It is found only where
> starfire was once worked. It holds no magic. It is simply the finest feedstock there is for
> the printworks that raise a foundry in minutes.
> Its seams lie in facing pairs, as if cast from one pour. And when the Host's surveyors mapped
> them, they found that every seam on Vesper runs back toward a single origin.
> Their conclusion was short. The Starforge is here.

Shots:

- 6.1 (7 s) A green and grey world from orbit, half in shadow, the blue-white star beyond its edge.
- 6.2 (8 s) Moorland in summer: birches, spruce, a lake with reeds; an eagle circling; a wolf pack crossing a meadow.
- 6.3 (8 s) A smallholding a century ago: dry-stone field walls, a stone farmhouse, smoke from the chimney, a family at work.
- 6.4 (8 s) The Clearance: the same family on the road with a cart, soldiers of the Host standing by, the door of the house left open.
- 6.5 (7 s) Years later: the same house roofless, a birch growing through the floor, a rusted mining rig abandoned in the field.
- 6.6 (8 s) Close on a seam: pale teal crystal shards in dark rock, veins of light deep inside, the whole seam brightening and dimming slowly like breath.
- 6.7 (7 s) Two seams on facing hillsides, mirror images of each other.
- 6.8 (7 s) Surveyors in field armour with tripods and lamps on a ridge at dusk. One draws lines on a chart (no readable marks); every line converges on one point. He looks up at the star.

#### 7. The split Muster and the Silence (6:00-7:05)

Narration:

> The Muster was asked what should be done, and it split four ways. The split fell along the
> wings.
> Blue would find the Starforge and relight it. The purposed things are failing one by one, they
> say, and a people has a right to its inheritance. They are called Kindlers.
> Red would find it and seal it cold, under both seals. The fire went home, they say, and to
> take it down a second time is theft. They are called Keepers.
> Green would carry it whole to Matins, under both seals, for the two hands to decide together.
> They are called Bearers.
> Yellow would give it back to the orders, who guarded it before there was an Empire. They are
> called Returners.
> Each suspects the worst of the others.
> Either the Khan or the Holy Mother could end this with a sentence. Neither has spoken. No wing
> has been named rebel. Blue and Red landed on Vesper first, and Green and Yellow a season later,
> each as a lawful host of the Empire, to secure the site.
> So in law there is no civil war. There is a disputed custody.

Shots:

- 7.1 (9 s) A round stone council chamber full of house-captains. They rise and divide to the four quarters of the floor, blue, red, green and yellow cloaks, leaving the centre bare.
- 7.2 (8 s) A captain in blue holds up an old lamp of Maker's work, dark and dead, and offers it to the chamber.
- 7.3 (8 s) A captain in red lays his open hand flat on a covered stone hearth, as if keeping a lid down.
- 7.4 (8 s) The Khan's high seat in the Camp, the man on it motionless, a line of messengers waiting unheard at the door.
- 7.5 (8 s) The Mother Hall. The veiled Matriarch motionless under the opening in the roof. A lamp burns down beside her.
- 7.6 (7 s) A sheet on a table with two empty circles where seals should be. Dust settles on it.
- 7.7 (9 s) Two fleets of drop-ships, one marked blue, one orange-red, descending through cloud onto the same moorland from opposite horizons.
- 7.8 (8 s) First contact: muzzle flashes across a valley at dusk, seen from far away, small and silent.

#### 8. The orders, the cores, the gantry (7:05-8:15)

Narration:

> One thing more stands above this war.
> The knightly orders are older than the Empire and older than the faith. They descend from the
> guards of the Makers, and they hold what remains of the Makers' greatest work. At the heart of
> each of their war machines sits a core: a purposed mind, forged in the Bright Age. Nobody can
> make another. Since the Quenching a core can think, remember and advise. It cannot move a limb
> without a sworn human hand.
> The orders swear to neither Khan nor Mother, and to no wing. But by ancient right, any lawful
> host that raises a gantry is owed one knight and one core. Once. And because every wing is
> lawful, the orders answer all of them.
> Your knight will not take your orders. He will tell you what he sees. The quiet sayings he
> passes on, the orders say, are the core's, and only he hears it. Whether that is so, the orders
> decline to prove.

Shots:

- 8.1 (8 s) Orbit over Vesper. Dark, plain ships hang in a loose line. On their hulls, simple painted devices: a white ring, an unlit lantern, a broken crown, a single ember, a hammer.
- 8.2 (9 s) A vault aboard ship. An old dark-metal object the size of a barrel rests in a cradle, a faint steady light behind its grille like a banked ember. Lamps, not screens.
- 8.3 (8 s) A knight in plain dark armour kneels and rests a bare hand on the grille. The light inside does not change.
- 8.4 (8 s) Fitters swarm a modern walking frame in a launch bay, swinging weapons onto its arms in haste. A sealed, unlit casket is lowered into its chest and the plates close.
- 8.5 (8 s) On the ground at the edge of a blue-marked base: a steel gantry beside a landing pad, floodlit, empty, waiting.
- 8.6 (12 s) The drop: a nine-metre war machine falls through dusk cloud on retro-rockets, lands in a blast of dust beside the gantry, straightens. A cloth standard unfurls from its back.
- 8.7 (9 s) Scale: soldiers in blue-shouldered iron look up at it from knee height. It turns its head toward the far ridge, not toward them.
- 8.8 (8 s) Far across the valley, in the red camp, an identical gantry, and a second machine coming down.

#### 9. The war that eats its own map (8:15-9:20)

Narration:

> And so, the war. There is no front line. There are a hundred small fields along the seams,
> each wing following the ore toward an origin none has reached.
> You will have noticed the difficulty. The seams are the only map to the Starforge, and every
> wing digs them up to print tanks. This war eats its own map.
> One caution, from my own office. Before the split, the Annalists kept a file on every officer
> of the Host. When the wings parted, each took its share of the archive, and all of the habit. The
> officer across the field from you is served by a staff that studied you in peacetime. They
> see only what their own soldiers see. But they amend your file after every engagement.
> Fight the same battle twice, and they will be waiting for it.

Shots:

- 9.1 (8 s) A squat tracked mining machine cuts into a teal seam with a spinning cutter; its hopper windows fill with teal light. Drums begin, low.
- 9.2 (8 s) A foundry: the ore goes in; beside it a building rises out of the ground layer by layer, printed, in seconds.
- 9.3 (7 s) Heavy tanks roll out in a column past the still-glowing seam.
- 9.4 (7 s) The seam, mined out: its light gives up in a last burst of motes and goes dark.
- 9.5 (8 s) The surveyor's chart from above: the converging lines fade from the paper one by one, from the outside in.
- 9.6 (9 s) Battle at dusk: blue and red tracer across a meadow, infantry bounding between low stone field walls, a spruce wood catching fire, wolves running from it.
- 9.7 (8 s) An archive: endless shelves of bound files split by a bare aisle, every second shelf empty.
- 9.8 (10 s) Lamplight. A file open on a desk (no readable text), a gloved hand adding a line to it. Beyond the desk, a table model of a battlefield with small blue and red markers; a hand moves one red marker to where a blue one stood.

#### 10. What the surveyors suspect; title (9:20-10:00)

Narration:

> A few surveyors hold one more opinion, and it is not in any report. Twin seams, from one pour,
> all running back to one place. They wonder whether the teal does not point to the Starforge at
> all. Whether it is the Starforge: broken on the night of the Quenching, and scattered.
> If they are right, there is nothing to find, and every wing is feeding it into its
> foundries by the tonne.
> That is not your concern, Commander. Your field is waiting. The first star is up.
> They are already singing.

Shots:

- 10.1 (9 s) A surveyor alone on a ridge at dusk holds a shard of teal crystal up against the evening star. The veins of light inside it are white-gold for an instant, then teal again.
- 10.2 (6 s) A foundry hopper: teal ore pouring in by the tonne.
- 10.3 (13 s) The valley of the key art: blue camp, red camp, two lakes, crystals glowing in the foreground rocks, a war machine's silhouette on the right ridge, the Brightest Star above. Chant from both camps, slightly out of step. Slow push in. Hold.
- 10.4 (6 s) Fade the picture to near black, the star remaining. (In the edit: the quoted line, small and pale: "Steel does not possess courage. It possesses purpose.")
- 10.5 (6 s) (In the edit: the title STARFORGE, and under it ADAPTIVE WARFARE.) The star fades last.
