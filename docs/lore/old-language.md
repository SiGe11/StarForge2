# THE OLD LANGUAGE (Highspeche)

> Companion to [starforge-lore.md](starforge-lore.md), and canon like it. Any text in the Old Language (an oath, a motto, an inscription, a title, a phrase in a core's line) follows this file. A word not in the lexicon is added here first, then used. Changes are the author's decision.

## 1. Two languages
- **The Vulgar** (*Folkspeche*, "folk speech") is what everyone speaks: at home, in the halls between offices, in the courts' hearings, on the field net. In the game it is plain modern English.
- **The Old Language** (*Highspeche*, "high speech") is the language of the Bright Age, from which the Vulgar grew during the Long Night, as Italian grew from Latin. Nobody speaks it at home and nobody has for centuries. It is read, written, sung and sworn in. The Luminary calls it **the High Speech**; everyone else says the Old Language. The two names mean the same tongue.

**The aim:** an English speaker should understand it about as well as an Italian understands Latin. Most words are recognisable when read slowly, and the endings and word order are foreign. *Brighteste Sterrethe under* is "under the brightest star": the words are familiar, but the article sits on the end of the noun and "under" comes after it.

**Who knows it**
- **The clergy**, fluently. Every Mother and celebrant reads and sings it, and the Matriarch preaches in it.
- **The knightly orders.** They keep their hours in it by their own older use, and knights quote it.
- **The Annalists and the courts.** Acts given at the Star, court judgements and the Annalists' formal records are drawn up in it.
- **Educated people**, a little. The halls' schools teach the prayers and the set phrases. Nearly everyone can follow a liturgy, and few can make a sentence.

**Where it is used**
- the Great Liturgy and the hours;
- the Host-oath (soldiers swear words they half understand);
- mottos and inscriptions (the orders, the halls, the coin);
- formal names and titles;
- acts given at the Star;
- very rarely, a phrase in a core's quiet line (section 8).

## 2. Its feel
In vocabulary it is mostly English, in old and middle forms, with a layer of Latin for the liturgy and the law and a few words each from Icelandic, Greek and Hungarian (the lexicon in section 10 is the count). In grammar roughly 30% English, 25% Latin, 25% Hungarian, 10% Greek and 10% Icelandic. The grammar's shares are for feel, not counting.
- **Words: from English.** Old and Middle English forms an English reader can sound out: *light*, *oath*, *knight*, *iron*, *smithe* smithy, *sterre* star, *warra* war, *eld* age, *stilnesse* silence.
- **Words: from Icelandic.** A few roots: *heim*, *himin*; and the article and the middle voice's *-sk* (grammar, below).
- **Words: from Hungarian.** A small layer of rare liturgical words, written the English way: *chillagwater*, star-water (from *csillag*, star; the *cs* is written *ch*, which an English reader says as the Hungarian does). Nothing outside the rite takes these.
- **Words: from Latin.** The liturgy and the law (*Vespera*, *Vigilia*, *Compact*, *sigil*, *regol*, *lanse*).
- **Words: from Greek.** A few learned words (*eikon* picture, *cleros* clergy).
- **Grammar: from Hungarian.** Endings stacked on the noun for place and motion, vowel harmony on those endings, definite and indefinite verbs, postpositions, and the dropped "is".
- **Grammar: from Latin.** Three genders shown by agreement, the accusative in *-m*, and the free word order with the verb last.
- **Grammar: from Greek.** The dual for pairs, the optative for blessings, and the middle voice.
- **Grammar: from Icelandic.** The article suffixed to the noun, and the middle voice's *-sk*.
- **Grammar: from English.** The verb endings (*-est*, *-eth*), the past in *-de*, the participles in *-ed*, *-en* and *-ende*, and the suffixes' own words (*-to*, *-in*, *-ut*, *-with*).

## 3. Sounds and spelling
**Nearly English** (the author's decision, 2026-11-24): the Old Language is written so that an English reader sounds it out at sight. Latin letters, as English uses them, with ö and ü in a few endings and Ó in the word of address. Older copies wrote þ and ð for *th*, æ for *a* or *ae*, *hw* for *wh*, *k* for the hard *c* and *ht* for *ght*; those spellings are not used.

| Letter | Sound |
|---|---|
| th | *th* in "thin" or "this", as in English |
| wh | *wh* in "which" |
| gh | as in English "light", "knight" (silent after i) |
| ch | *ch* in "church" |
| c | always hard, as in "cat" (*Compact*, *Camp*, *clocca*) |
| k | hard, where English writes k, and before e and i where c would read soft (*ketel*, *kin*, *kindlan*, *kern*, *waker*) |
| ae | *a* in "cat", held a little long (*daeg*) |
| g | always hard, as in "get" |
| s | always as in "sun" |
| ö, ü | German ö and ü |
| Ó | a long *o*; only the word of address (section 4) |
| other letters | as in English; a doubled letter where an ending meets a stem is kept (*Lighttheinto*: light-the-into) |

**Stress** is always on the first syllable, with a lighter beat on every second syllable after it. That is why chant in the Old Language sounds even and heavy.

**Vowel harmony**, only on noun and adjective endings. Vowels fall into three groups.
- **Back:** a, o, u.
- **Front:** e, ö, ü.
- **Neutral:** i, y.

An ending that comes in two forms takes the back form when the **stem's** last non-neutral vowel is back, and the front form otherwise: *sunna* (sun) but *sterre* (star); *hosttheto* (to the Host) but *hevedtö* (to a head). A stem with only neutral vowels takes the front form. The article, the possessives and verbs do not change and do not count. The nearly-English spelling moved a few stems, and their endings with them: war is *warra* (its *a* is back, so the feminine ending is *-a*), while star and watch keep a front stem (*sterre*, *wecche*); light and knight have only the neutral *i*, so they take the front form (*lighttö*, *knighttö*).

## 4. Nouns
**Three genders**, shown by agreeing adjectives and by the pronoun (he, heo, hit), as in Latin. Gender is grammar and says nothing about the person: *soldar* is masculine whoever wears the iron.

| Gender | Usual dictionary form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | bare stem | *knight*, *field*, *oath*, *hamor* |
| feminine | stem + **-a / -e** (harmony) | *sunna* sun, *sterre* star, *warra* war |
| neuter | bare stem | *light*, *iron*, *fire*, *work* |

A few old feminines end in a consonant: *hand*, *night*, *eld*, *tid*, *moder*, *suster*, and every noun in *-ung* (*offrung*, *Quenchung*, *Kindlung*). Latin and Greek loans keep their own forms (*Vespera*, *Vigilia*, *eikon*).

**Building a noun.** The stem is the dictionary form without the feminine -a/-e, and harmony goes by it. The -a/-e itself drops only before an ending that begins with a vowel (*sterr-en*, stars) and stays before one that begins with a consonant (*sterre-the*, the star; *sunna-the*, the sun). Add, in this order:

**STEM + number + (article OR possessive) + case**

**Number**
- Singular adds nothing.
- **Dual** (exactly two, a pair) adds **-twa**: *handtwa* (two hands), *seamtwa* (twin seams).
- **Plural** adds **-an / -en**, the old English plural of "oxen": *knight-en*, *sterr-en*, *sunn-an*.

**The article** is "the" put on the end: **-the** in the singular and **-tha** in the dual and plural, the same for every gender.
- *sterre* a star, *sterrethe* the star.
- *knighten* knights, *knightentha* the knights.
- *handtwatha* the two hands.

**Possessives** take the article's place: -min (my), -thin (thy), -his (his, its), -hir (her), -ure (our), -yur (your), -heore (their). *oathmin* my oath, *wingheore* their wing.

**Cases.** The accusative and genitive are short endings. The rest are English prepositions turned into endings, Hungarian-fashion.

| Case | Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | none | subject | *sterrethe* the star |
| accusative | -m; after a consonant -am / -em | object | *oathminam* my oath (object) |
| genitive | -s; after a consonant -as / -es | of, -'s | *sterrethes* the star's |
| dative | -to / -tö | to, for | *hosttheto* to the Host |
| inessive | -in | in | *hallthein* in the hall |
| illative | -into | into | *lighttheinto* into the light |
| elative | -ut / -üt | out of | *Camptheut* out of the Camp |
| superessive | -on / -ön | on | *anviltheon* on the anvil |
| sublative | -onto / -öntö | onto | *altartheonto* onto the altar |
| delative | -of / -öf | off, down from | *tortheof* down from the gantry |
| adessive | -bi | at, by, beside | *Sterrethebi* at the Star |
| allative | -ward / -werd | toward | *Matinsward* toward Matins |
| ablative | -fram / -frem | from | *fieldthefrem* from the field |
| instrumental | -with | with, by means of | *handthewith* by the hand |
| terminative | -til | up to, until | *Laudestil* until lauds |

**Address:** **Ó** before the nominative: *Ó Sterre*.

**Postpositions** follow a nominative noun, as in Hungarian: *under*, *ofer* (over), *bitwene* (between), *bifore* (before, for the sake of), *after*, *beyondan* (beyond). *Brighteste Sterrethe under* means "under the brightest star".

**The genitive comes before** what it owns, as in English: *Sterrethes handtwa*, "the Star's two hands".

## 5. Adjectives
- They stand before the noun and agree with it: masculine bare, feminine **-a / -e**, neuter **-t** (except after t or d), plural and dual **-e**.
- **They take no case**, as in Hungarian: *Brighteste Sterrethebi*, "at the brightest star".
- The comparative is **-er** and the superlative **-est**, as in English, before the agreement ending: *bright*, *brighter*, *Brighteste Sterre*.
- **Past participles** act as adjectives and agree: *purposed thing* (a purposed thing), *settede eld* (an appointed age).
- **Present participles** in **-ende** never change: *burnende* burning, *aptende* adapting.

## 7. Verbs
The dictionary form is the infinitive in **-an**; drop it for the stem: *swearan* (swear) gives *swear-*, *holdan* (hold) gives *hold-*. Verb endings never change for harmony.

| Person | Present | Past (add -d- or -de-) |
|---|---|---|
| I | -e | -de |
| thou | -est | -dest |
| he, she, it | -eth | -de |
| we | -um | -dum |
| you | -ith | -dith |
| they | -ant | -dant |
| we two (dual) | -wit | -dwit |
| you two (dual) | -git | -dgit |
| they two (dual) | -twa | -dtwa |

*Ik sweare* (I swear), *he holdeth* (he holds), *we sweardum* (we swore).

A stem that ends in *d* or *t* takes a linking *-e-* before the past endings: *ik holdede* (I held), *we tendedum* (we tended). Strong verbs are strong only in their past participle; their past tense is regular (*we sweardum*).

**Definite and indefinite**, as in Hungarian. When the object is definite (it has the article or a possessive, or it is a name or him, her, it or them), the verb adds **-it** after its ending: *ik sweare oatham* (I swear an oath), *ik sweareit oathminam* (I swear my oath, literally "I swear-it"). Only finite verbs take it: an infinitive or an imperative stays as it is (*tidentham holdan*, *Tend hem*).

**Future:** *shal* plus the infinitive at the end of the clause. *Shal* goes *shal, shalt, shal, shalum, shalith, shalant*: *Ik oatham shal swearan*.

**Imperative:** the bare stem in the singular (*Swear!* *Hold!*) and the stem + -ith in the plural (*Holdith!*).

**Optative** (Greek), for blessings and solemn wishes, never for requests (the light is received, not petitioned): stem + **-oi**. The liturgy's closing response is *Beoi*, "so be it".

**Middle voice** (Greek meaning, Icelandic form): **-sk** after the full form, for acting on oneself, for each other, or being done to. *he swearethsk* (he binds himself by oath), *light bearethsk* (light is carried), *hit kindlethsk* (it kindles itself).

**Participles**
- Present: **-ende** (*burnende*).
- Past: **-ed** for most verbs (*blessed*, *loosed*, *purposed*).
- Past of the strong verbs: **-en** (*given*, *sworen*, *boren*, *gangen*, *broken*), often with a changed vowel; the lexicon gives each one.

**To be** (*beon*) is irregular, as in English: *am, art, is; arum, arith, arant*; past *was, wast, was; warum, warith, warant*. **"Is" and "are" are dropped in the present** (the third person only), as in Hungarian: *Firethe ded*, "the fire (is) dead"; *Kernentha laste embren*, "the cores (are) the last embers".

**Negation:** *ne* before the verb (*ne gangan*, not to go). **Question:** *-ne* attached to the first word (*Swearestne?*, "dost thou swear?").

## 8. Word order
The endings carry the meaning, so order carries emphasis.
- **Ordinary prose** puts the subject first and the verb last: *Ik oatham sweare* (I swear an oath).
- **The word just before the verb is the focus**, as in Hungarian: *Hir oatham sweareit* means "it is HER oath I swear".
- **Liturgy and oaths** put the verb last and pile up their phrases before it.
- **Mottos** drop the verb when they can.

**In a core's quiet line** (rare): at most one short phrase, never a whole line, and the line must read correctly without it. Since only the knight hears the core, the phrase reaches the Commander in the knight's voice, untranslated. No more than one such line a match.

## 9. Set texts
Each is given in the Old Language, then glossed word by word, then in the Vulgar.

**The Host-oath** (sworn by every soldier, repeated after a celebrant):
> *Brighteste Sterrethe under, handthewith which holdeth and handthewith which blesseth, oathminam hosttheto sweareit: wingminin standan, tidentham holdan, fieldthefrem ne gangan, til loosed am.*

The words, in order:
- *Brighteste Sterrethe under*: brightest star-the under.
- *handthewith which holdeth*: hand-the-with which holds.
- *and handthewith which blesseth*: and hand-the-with which blesses.
- *oathminam hosttheto sweareit*: oath-my-OBJ host-the-to swear-I-it.
- *wingminin standan*: wing-my-in to stand.
- *tidentham holdan*: hours-the-OBJ to keep.
- *fieldthefrem ne gangan*: field-the-from not to go.
- *til loosed am*: until released I am.

"Under the Brightest Star, by the hand that holds and the hand that blesses, I swear my oath to the Host: to stand in my wing, to keep the hours, and not to go from the field until I am released."

**Given at the Star** (dating an act under both seals): *Sterrethebi given*, "given at the Star" (the participle last, as in *Lighttheinto gangen*).

**Vespers, at the first star:**
> Celebrant: *Firste sterrethe waketh.* "The first star wakes."
> People: *Lightthem takumit.* (light-the-OBJ take-we-it) "We receive the light."
> All: *Beoi.* "So be it."

**On a grave:** *Lighttheinto gangen*: "gone into the light".

**The two hands, in full titles:**
- *Kan, hand which holdeth*: "Khan, the hand that holds".
- *Holiga Moder, hand which blesseth*: "Holy Mother, the hand that blesses".
- The reigning pair: *Temrik Fourth* (Temrik IV) and *Aveline Thirde* (Aveline III).

**The orders' mottos:**

| Order | Motto | Words | Vulgar |
|---|---|---|---|
| the Ashen Covenant | *Firethe ded. Mourn and ward.* | fire-the dead. mourn and guard. | The fire is dead. Mourn and guard. |
| the Order of the Iron Lantern | *Light bearethsk, ne bidethsk.* | light is-borne, not is-bided | Light is carried, not awaited. |
| the Ninth Vigil | *Ninthe wecche. Ne loosede.* | ninth watch. not released | The ninth watch. Not released. |
| the Sable Oath | *Oath eikonam ne needeth.* | oath picture-OBJ not needs | An oath needs no picture. |
| the Order of the Broken Crown | *Crowned hevedtö ne serveth.* | crowned head-to not serves | It serves no crowned head. |
| the Emberguard | *Kernentha laste embren. Tend hem.* | cores-the last embers. tend them | The cores are the last embers. Tend them. |
| the Wardens of the Long Night | *Nightthe settede eld. Stand wel.* | night-the appointed age. stand well | The Night is the appointed age. Stand well. |
| the Brazen Host | *Workthe offrung.* | work-the offering | The work is the offering. |

**The title's line:** *Sterrethe under*, "under the Star": the Host-oath's opening, *Brighteste Sterrethe under*, without its adjective. Every soldier knows the words. (The war college keeps its motto in the Vulgar: "Adaptive Warfare".)

**The coin:** a star's face reads *Brighteste Sterre*; its back reads the Khan's name and *Kan*.

**The craft's last saying, as the Old Language would have it:** *Steel hertem ne haveth. Purposam haveth.* (steel heart-OBJ not has. purpose-OBJ has): "Steel does not possess courage. It possesses purpose."

Five usages those pages rely on: a ruler's ordinal is bare in the masculine (*Temrik First*), feminine in *-e* (*Aveline Othere*), and, unlike other adjectives, takes the case of the name before it (*Temrik Firstem*, *Aveline Firstes*); the delative (*-of / -öf*) also means "about, concerning", as Hungarian's does (*Handtheof*, "of the hand", as a heading); the demonstratives *this* and *yon* take the article's place (*yon nightin*, "in that night"); a numeral above two takes the plural (*nine wecchen*); a numeral takes no agreement or case (*an anvilon*, "on one anvil").

**Proper names** keep their Vulgar spelling: Vesper, Matins, Terce, Harrow, Sext, Nones, and the orders' names. The Vulgar's hour-names (vespers, lauds, compline) are this language's words worn down.

## 11. Rules for writers
1. **Knights' calls, soldiers' reports, the interface and tips are always in the Vulgar.** The Old Language appears only where section 1 says it is used.
2. **Gloss it** in player-facing text: the Old Language in italics, then the Vulgar after a dash, the first time it appears on a screen. *Beoi* and *Sterrethebi given* need no gloss after the Field Manual has given it.
3. **Build every word from this file**, and keep the test: an English reader should recognise the root. Check the stem, the gender, the harmony of the ending and the suffix order. A new word goes into the lexicon in the same change, with its gender.
4. **The Old Language answers nothing in the lore's section 11** ([starforge-lore.md](starforge-lore.md)). An Old Language text may be as ambiguous as the Vulgar, never more revealing.
5. **Fonts:** ö, ü and Ó must be checked in every UI font that shows the Old Language before any of it is put on screen.
