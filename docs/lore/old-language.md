# THE OLD LANGUAGE (Hihspæce)

> Companion to [starforge-lore.md](starforge-lore.md), and canon like it. Any text in the Old Language (an oath, a motto, an inscription, a title, a phrase in a core's line) follows this file. A word not in the lexicon is added here first, then used. Changes are the author's decision.

## 1. Two languages
- **The Vulgar** (*Folkspæce*, "folk speech") is what everyone speaks: at home, in the halls between offices, in the courts' hearings, on the field net. In the game it is plain modern English.
- **The Old Language** (*Hihspæce*, "high speech") is the language of the Bright Age, from which the Vulgar grew during the Long Night, as Italian grew from Latin. Nobody speaks it at home and nobody has for centuries. It is read, written, sung and sworn in. The Luminary calls it **the High Speech**; everyone else says the Old Language. The two names mean the same tongue.

**The aim:** an English speaker should understand it about as well as an Italian understands Latin. Most words are recognisable when read slowly, and the endings and word order are foreign. *Brihteste Sterreþe under* is "under the brightest star": the words are familiar, but the article sits on the end of the noun and "under" comes after it.

**Who knows it**
- **The clergy**, fluently. Every Mother and celebrant reads and sings it, and the Matriarch preaches in it.
- **The knightly orders.** They keep their hours in it by their own older use, and knights quote it.
- **The Annalists and the courts.** Acts given at the Star, court judgements and the Annalists' formal records are drawn up in it.
- **Educated people**, a little. The halls' schools teach the prayers and the set phrases. Nearly everyone can follow a liturgy, and few can make a sentence.

**Where it is used**
- the Great Liturgy and the hours;
- the Host-oath (soldiers swear words they half understand);
- mottos and inscriptions (the orders, the halls, the coin, the war college);
- formal names and titles;
- acts given at the Star;
- very rarely, a phrase in a core's quiet line (section 8).

## 2. Its feel
In vocabulary roughly 60% English, 15% Icelandic, 10% Latin and 5% Greek. In grammar roughly 30% English, 25% Latin, 25% Hungarian, 10% Greek and 10% Icelandic. These shares are for feel, not counting.
- **Words: from English.** Old and Middle English forms an English reader can sound out: *sterre* star, *liht* light, *oþ* oath, *kniht*, *iren*, *werre* war, *eld* age, *stilnesse* silence.
- **Words: from Icelandic.** The spelling (þ, ð, æ, k for the hard c) and a few roots: *heim*, *himin*.
- **Words: from Latin.** The liturgy and the law (*Vespera*, *Vigilia*, *Kompakt*, *sigil*, *regol*, *lance*).
- **Words: from Greek.** A few learned words (*eikon* picture, *kleros* clergy).
- **Grammar: from Hungarian.** Endings stacked on the noun for place and motion, vowel harmony on those endings, definite and indefinite verbs, postpositions, and the dropped "is".
- **Grammar: from Latin.** Three genders shown by agreement, the accusative in *-m*, and the free word order with the verb last.
- **Grammar: from Greek.** The dual for pairs, the optative for blessings, and the middle voice.
- **Grammar: from Icelandic.** The article suffixed to the noun, and the middle voice's *-sk*.
- **Grammar: from English.** The verb endings (*-est*, *-eþ*), the past in *-de*, the participles in *-ed*, *-en* and *-ende*, and the suffixes' own words (*-to*, *-in*, *-ut*, *-wiþ*).

## 3. Sounds and spelling
Latin letters, with þ, ð and æ, and ö and ü in a few endings.

| Letter | Sound |
|---|---|
| þ | *th* in "thin" |
| ð | *th* in "this" |
| æ | *a* in "cat", held a little long |
| k | always hard (the Old Language writes no c) |
| g | always hard, as in "get" |
| s | always as in "sun" |
| ö, ü | German ö and ü |
| j | *y* in "yes" |
| other letters | as in English, every letter said (*kniht* is "k-nikht") |

**Stress** is always on the first syllable, with a lighter beat on every second syllable after it. That is why chant in the Old Language sounds even and heavy.

**Vowel harmony**, only on noun and adjective endings. Vowels fall into three groups.
- **Back:** a, o, u.
- **Front:** e, æ, ö, ü.
- **Neutral:** i, y.

An ending that comes in two forms takes the back form when the **stem's** last non-neutral vowel is back, and the front form otherwise: *sunna* (sun) but *sterre* (star); *hostþeto* (to the Host) but *hevedtö* (to a head). A stem with only neutral vowels takes the front form. The article, the possessives and verbs do not change and do not count.

## 4. Nouns
**Three genders**, shown by agreeing adjectives and by the pronoun (he, heo, hit), as in Latin. Gender is grammar and says nothing about the person: *soldar* is masculine whoever wears the iron.

| Gender | Usual dictionary form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | bare stem | *kniht*, *feld*, *oþ*, *hamor* |
| feminine | stem + **-a / -e** (harmony) | *sunna* sun, *sterre* star, *werre* war |
| neuter | bare stem | *liht*, *iren*, *fyr*, *werk* |

A few old feminines end in a consonant: *hand*, *niht*, *eld*, *tid*, *moder*, and every noun in *-ung* (*offrung*, *Kwenkung*, *Kindlung*). Latin and Greek loans keep their own forms (*Vespera*, *Vigilia*, *eikon*).

**Building a noun.** Drop the feminine -a/-e to get the stem, then add, in this order:

**STEM + number + (article OR possessive) + case**

**Number**
- Singular adds nothing.
- **Dual** (exactly two, a pair) adds **-twa**: *handtwa* (two hands), *seamtwa* (twin seams).
- **Plural** adds **-an / -en**, the old English plural of "oxen": *kniht-en*, *sterr-en*, *sunn-an*.

**The article** is "the" put on the end: **-þe** in the singular and **-þa** in the dual and plural, the same for every gender.
- *sterre* a star, *sterreþe* the star.
- *knihten* knights, *knihtenþa* the knights.
- *handtwaþa* the two hands.

**Possessives** take the article's place: -min (my), -þin (thy), -his (his, its), -hir (her), -ure (our), -iuer (your), -heore (their). *oþmin* my oath, *wingheore* their wing.

**Cases.** The accusative and genitive are short endings. The rest are English prepositions turned into endings, Hungarian-fashion.

| Case | Ending | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| nominative | none | subject | *sterreþe* the star |
| accusative | -m; after a consonant -am / -em | object | *oþminam* my oath (object) |
| genitive | -s; after a consonant -as / -es | of, -'s | *sterreþes* the star's |
| dative | -to / -tö | to, for | *hostþeto* to the Host |
| inessive | -in | in | *halþein* in the hall |
| illative | -into | into | *lihtþeinto* into the light |
| elative | -ut / -üt | out of | *Kampþeut* out of the Camp |
| superessive | -on / -ön | on | *anvilþeon* on the anvil |
| sublative | -onto / -öntö | onto | *altarþeonto* onto the altar |
| delative | -of / -öf | off, down from | *torþeof* down from the gantry |
| adessive | -bi | at, by, beside | *Sterreþebi* at the Star |
| allative | -ward / -werd | toward | *Matinsward* toward Matins |
| ablative | -fram / -frem | from | *feldþefrem* from the field |
| instrumental | -wiþ | with, by means of | *handþewiþ* by the hand |
| terminative | -til | up to, until | *Laudestil* until lauds |

**Address:** **Ó** before the nominative: *Ó Sterre*.

**Postpositions** follow a nominative noun, as in Hungarian: *under*, *ofer* (over), *bitwene* (between), *bifore* (before, for the sake of), *æfter*, *bigeondan* (beyond). *Brihteste Sterreþe under* means "under the brightest star".

**The genitive comes before** what it owns, as in English: *Sterreþes handtwa*, "the Star's two hands".

## 5. Adjectives
- They stand before the noun and agree with it: masculine bare, feminine **-a / -e**, neuter **-t** (except after t or d), plural and dual **-e**.
- **They take no case**, as in Hungarian: *Brihteste Sterreþebi*, "at the brightest star".
- The comparative is **-er** and the superlative **-est**, as in English, before the agreement ending: *briht*, *brihter*, *Brihteste Sterre*.
- **Past participles** act as adjectives and agree: *purposed þing* (a purposed thing), *settede eld* (an appointed age).
- **Present participles** in **-ende** never change: *bernende* burning, *aptende* adapting.

|---|---|---|---|
| I | ik | me | min |
| thou | þu | þe | þin |
| he | he | him | his |
| she | heo | hir | hir |

For other cases, add the ending to the object form: *mewiþ* (with me), *usto* (to us).

Other small words:
- *hwich*: who, which, that (relative, never changes).
- *þæt*: that (introducing a clause).
- *and*, *or*, *but*, *gif* (if), *til* (until), *þan* (then), *nu* (now), *æfre* (ever), *næfre* (never), *wel* (well), *swa* (so).

## 7. Verbs
The dictionary form is the infinitive in **-an**; drop it for the stem: *sweran* (swear) gives *swer-*, *haldan* (hold) gives *hald-*. Verb endings never change for harmony.

| Person | Present | Past (add -d- or -de-) |
|---|---|---|
| I | -e | -de |
| thou | -est | -dest |
| he, she, it | -eþ | -de |
| we | -um | -dum |
| you | -iþ | -diþ |
| they | -ant | -dant |
| we two (dual) | -wit | -dwit |
| you two (dual) | -git | -dgit |
| they two (dual) | -twa | -dtwa |

*Ik swere* (I swear), *he haldeþ* (he holds), *we swerdum* (we swore).

**Definite and indefinite**, as in Hungarian. When the object is definite (it has the article or a possessive, or it is a name or him, her, it or them), the verb adds **-it** after its ending: *ik swere oþam* (I swear an oath), *ik swereit oþminam* (I swear my oath, literally "I swear-it").

**Future:** *shal* plus the infinitive at the end of the clause. *Shal* goes *shal, shalt, shal, shalum, shaliþ, shalant*: *Ik oþam shal sweran*.

**Imperative:** the bare stem in the singular (*Swer!* *Hald!*) and the stem + -iþ in the plural (*Haldiþ!*).

**Optative** (Greek), for blessings and solemn wishes, never for requests (the light is received, not petitioned): stem + **-oi**. The liturgy's closing response is *Beoi*, "so be it".

**Middle voice** (Greek meaning, Icelandic form): **-sk** after the full form, for acting on oneself, for each other, or being done to. *he swereþsk* (he binds himself by oath), *liht bereþsk* (light is carried), *hit kindleþsk* (it kindles itself).

**Participles**
- Present: **-ende** (*bernende*).
- Past: **-ed** for most verbs (*blessed*, *losed*, *purposed*).
- Past of the strong verbs: **-en** (*given*, *sworen*, *boren*, *gangen*, *broken*).

**To be** (*beon*) is irregular, as in English: *am, art, is; arum, ariþ, arant*; past *was, wast, was; warum, wariþ, warant*. **"Is" is dropped in the present**, as in Hungarian: *Fyrþe ded*, "the fire (is) dead".

**Negation:** *ne* before the verb (*ne gangan*, not to go). **Question:** *-ne* attached to the first word (*Swerestne?*, "dost thou swear?").

## 8. Word order
The endings carry the meaning, so order carries emphasis.
- **Ordinary prose** puts the subject first and the verb last: *Ik oþam swere* (I swear an oath).
- **The word just before the verb is the focus**, as in Hungarian: *Hir oþam swereit* means "it is HER oath I swear".
- **Liturgy and oaths** put the verb last and pile up their phrases before it.
- **Mottos** drop the verb when they can.

**In a core's quiet line** (rare): at most one short phrase, never a whole line, and the line must read correctly without it. Since only the knight hears the core, the phrase reaches the Commander in the knight's voice, untranslated. No more than one such line a match.

## 9. Set texts
Each is given in the Old Language, then glossed word by word, then in the Vulgar.

**The Host-oath** (sworn by every soldier, repeated after a celebrant):
> *Brihteste Sterreþe under, handþewiþ hwich haldeþ and handþewiþ hwich blesseþ, oþminam hostþeto swereit: wingminin standan, tidenþam haldan, feldþefrem ne gangan, til losed am.*

The words, in order:
- *Brihteste Sterreþe under*: brightest star-the under.
- *handþewiþ hwich haldeþ*: hand-the-with which holds.
- *and handþewiþ hwich blesseþ*: and hand-the-with which blesses.
- *oþminam hostþeto swereit*: oath-my-OBJ host-the-to swear-I-it.
- *wingminin standan*: wing-my-in to stand.
- *tidenþam haldan*: hours-the-OBJ to keep.
- *feldþefrem ne gangan*: field-the-from not to go.
- *til losed am*: until released I am.

"Under the Brightest Star, by the hand that holds and the hand that blesses, I swear my oath to the Host: to stand in my wing, to keep the hours, and not to go from the field until I am released."

**Given at the Star** (dating an act under both seals): *Given Sterreþebi*, "given at the Star".

**Vespers, at the first star:**
> Celebrant: *Ferste sterre wakeþ.* "The first star wakes."
> People: *Lihtþem takumit.* (light-the-OBJ take-we-it) "We receive the light."
> All: *Beoi.* "So be it."

**On a grave:** *Lihtþeinto gangen*: "gone into the light".

**The two hands, in full titles:**
- *Kan, hand hwich haldeþ*: "Khan, the hand that holds".
- *Haliga Moder, hand hwich blesseþ*: "Holy Mother, the hand that blesses".
- The reigning pair: *Temrik Feorþ* (Temrik IV) and *Aveline Þridde* (Aveline III).

**The orders' mottos:**

| Order | Motto | Words | Vulgar |
|---|---|---|---|
| the Ashen Covenant | *Fyrþe ded. Murn and weard.* | fire-the dead. mourn and guard. | The fire is dead. Mourn and guard. |
| the Order of the Iron Lantern | *Liht bereþsk, ne bideþsk.* | light is-borne, not is-bided | Light is carried, not awaited. |
| the Ninth Vigil | *Nigoþa wæcce. Ne losed.* | ninth watch. not released | The ninth watch. Not released. |
| the Sable Oath | *Oþ eikonam ne neodeþ.* | oath picture-OBJ not needs | An oath needs no picture. |
| the Order of the Broken Crown | *Kruned hevedtö ne serveþ.* | crowned head-to not serves | It serves no crowned head. |
| the Emberguard | *Kernenþa laste embren. Tend hem.* | cores-the last embers. tend them | The cores are the last embers. Tend them. |
| the Wardens of the Long Night | *Nihtþe settede eld. Stand wel.* | night-the appointed age. stand well | The Night is the appointed age. Stand well. |
| the Brazen Host | *Werkþe offrung.* | work-the offering | The work is the offering. |

**The war college:** *Aptende Werre*, "adapting war", which is "Adaptive Warfare" in the Vulgar.

**The coin:** a star's face reads *Brihteste Sterre*; its back reads the Khan's name and *Kan*.

**The craft's last saying, as the Old Language would have it:** *Stel hertem ne habbeþ. Purposam habbeþ.* (steel heart-OBJ not has. purpose-OBJ has): "Steel does not possess courage. It possesses purpose."

## 11. Rules for writers
1. **Knights' calls, soldiers' reports, the interface and tips are always in the Vulgar.** The Old Language appears only where section 1 says it is used.
2. **Gloss it** in player-facing text: the Old Language in italics, then the Vulgar after a dash, the first time it appears on a screen. *Beoi* and *Given Sterreþebi* need no gloss after the Field Manual has given it.
3. **Build every word from this file**, and keep the test: an English reader should recognise the root. Check the stem, the gender, the harmony of the ending and the suffix order. A new word goes into the lexicon in the same change, with its gender.
4. **The Old Language answers nothing in the lore's section 11** ([starforge-lore.md](starforge-lore.md)). An Old Language text may be as ambiguous as the Vulgar, never more revealing.
5. **Fonts:** þ, ð, æ, ö and ü must be checked in the UI's fonts (Inter, Roboto Mono) before any of it is put on screen.
