# STARFORGE: WORLD LORE

> The canon for StarForge's story. Everything the game says or shows (text, names, speech, art, the intro) is checked against this file; the rules for writing are in section 13, the image and video prompts in `prompts.md` beside it. Nothing here contradicts the game's code, data or README: where a game rule the lore leans on changes, the line here changes with it. Changes to the lore itself are the author's decision.

## 1. In one paragraph
The Empire of the Brightest Star has kept its own peace for seven centuries. Its capital in law is the star itself, where nobody lives. It is governed in the star's name by two hands that may never become one: the Khan, who holds, and the Matriarch, the Holy Mother, who blesses and who elects each Khan. Long before the Empire, its ancestors worked **starfire**, a fire that gave made things a purpose of their own. That fire went out a thousand years ago. Now surveyors on the emptied farm-world of Vesper have found signs of the **Starforge**, the hearth where starfire was first worked. The Muster could not agree what to do with it, the two wings of the Imperial Host landed to "secure the site", and neither the Khan nor the Holy Mother has said a word. Two halves of one army, under one oath, are fighting over a thing no living person has seen.

## 2. The Bright Age and the Quenching
- **Starfire.** The old magic was never spells or sorcerers. It was a craft of making. A thing forged in starfire kept a **purpose**: a wall that meant to stand, a plough that meant to cut straight, a machine that meant to guard. The Mech's line "Steel does not possess courage. It possesses purpose." is the last living statement of that craft.
- **The Makers.** The smiths who worked starfire. They held that it was sunlight brought down to the anvil. No Maker's name survives, only their work.
- **The Starforge.** The first hearth, from which every other starfire was lit.
- **The Quenching.** In one night every starfire went out. Nine watches were kept over the Starforge that night, and the fire failed during the ninth. Purposed things kept their purpose but lost the power to act on it alone.
- **Three accounts of why**, none proven:
  - *The Luminary:* the fire was lent by the stars and has gone home. It still burns where it always burned. Look up.
  - *The Khan's chroniclers:* it was spent. The last Makers burned it all in a war nobody recorded.
  - *The orders:* the Makers put it out themselves, and the orders swore something that night which they do not discuss.
- **The Long Night.** The name of the present age. There is no magic in it, and nobody alive has met anyone who saw any.

## 3. The Empire of the Brightest Star
- **The capital.** The Brightest Star is a blue-white giant, the one light that stands in the night sky of every world the Empire holds. It has no habitable world and nobody lives there. Sovereignty is held to rest in the star itself, and the Khan and the Matriarch are its two hands.
- **"Given at the Star."** An act under both seals is dated "given at the Star" and binds the whole Empire. An act under one seal is dated from that hand's own seat and binds only that hand's sphere.
- **Founding.** Three centuries of house wars followed the Quenching. The first Matriarch ended them by electing one war-captain over all the others, on condition that he never rule the faith. That bargain is the **Compact**.
- **The Matriarch, the Holy Mother** (now Aveline III). Spiritual head, seated at the **Mother Hall**. Chosen for life by the senior Mothers of the Luminary; the Khan has no voice in it. She elects the Khan and is the only authority that can release a sworn oath.
- **The Khan** (now Temrik IV). Worldly head. Commands the Host, the courts and the treasury. His seat is a stone city still called **the Camp**, because the first Khans ruled from the saddle.
- **The Election.** When a Khan dies, the Matriarch keeps nine nights of vigil and names the successor at vespers on the ninth. The **Muster**, the assembly of house-captains, then swears to him. It has never refused, and the law does not say what happens if it does. The new Khan sails once to within sight of the Brightest Star to be invested, the only time a ruler visits the capital.
- **Limits on the Election.** She elects and never commands. A Khan once invested holds for life and cannot be unseated by her. She may not elect her own kin or anyone in the Luminary's service.
- **The rule of the Compact.** No one holds both hands. Matters of the sword take the Khan's seal, matters of the light take the Mother's, and matters of both must be given at the Star. The Starforge is the first matter in seven centuries that is wholly both.
- **The Imperial Host.** By old custom it is kept in two equal wings, **Blue** and **Red**, so that no captain ever commands all of it. Same establishment, same equipment, same oath, sworn under the Brightest Star: "by the hand that holds and the hand that blesses".

## 4. The Luminary and the Rite of Stars
- **From one sun to all stars.** The oldest cult was of a single sun, the Makers' sun. When the people spread to other worlds, each under a different sun, the cult widened: every sun is a star and every star a sun. The Quenching finished the turn. The light can no longer be worked, only received.
- **What is taught.** All light is one kindred. Each person is named under a **birth-star**, the star risen at their birth, and keeps its rising as their own feast. The Brightest Star is first among the kindred and holds the Empire. The dead are said to have "gone into the light"; the Luminary declines to say more.
- **The Luminary.** The body of the clergy. The Matriarch at its head, the **Mothers** over the provinces, and under them **celebrants** (women and men), cantors, lectors, acolytes and thurifers.
- **The halls.** A hall of the Rite is long, built of stone, and open to the sky above the altar through a round **oculus**. Its far end is aligned on the rising of the Brightest Star. A pierced screen hung with lamps and gold-ground star-icons stands between the people and the altar; its doors open only at the Taking of the Light.
- **The hours.** The day is kept in offices: **lauds** at sunrise to the world's own sun, which is the old sun-cult surviving in one short office; **vespers** at the first star; **compline** before sleep; **vigils** through the night on the eve of a feast.
- **The Great Liturgy.** Sung at night, in the High Speech that nobody has spoken for centuries, in unaccompanied chant. Celebrant and people face the same way, toward the altar and the Star. There are processions with lamps, incense from swung censers, hand bells, long silences, and kneeling.
- **Star-water.** Water that has stood a night under the open sky. Used to bless people, standards, thresholds and the dying.
- **Vestments.** A long white linen alb, girded with a cord. Over these a wide sleeveless mantle in the colour of the season, stiff with gold thread and worked with star-points. For vespers and processions, a great hooded cope clasped at the breast. Hands are covered with a silk shoulder-veil when carrying the mirror-dish. A Mother wears a tall closed crown set with a single stone and carries a staff topped with a lamp. The Matriarch alone is veiled in white from crown to floor.
- **Colours.** Gold for feasts, white for ordinary time, black for the Night of the Quenching. Blue and red were laid aside when the wings went to war and will not be worn again until the Host is one.
- **The Night of the Quenching.** Kept once a year. Black vestments, every lamp put out one by one during the chant, the hall left dark and silent until lauds.
- **In the field.** Both wings carry field celebrants. Vespers is sung at the first star in both camps, in the same words, sometimes within earshot.

## 5. The disruption
- **Teal.** Proper name **quenchstone**. It is found only on worlds where starfire was once worked, and scholars take it for the residue the fire left. It holds no magic. It is simply the best feedstock known for the printworks that raise a Foundry in minutes. Its seams lie in facing pairs, as if cast from one pour.
- **Vesper.** Named for the hour: from this world the Brightest Star is the evening star. A temperate world seeded with the old stock: birch, spruce, wolf, fox, eagle. Smallholders farmed it for centuries. A hundred years ago the Muster cleared them off for ore concessions (**the Clearance**). The concessions failed, the land went back to birch and wolves, and the field walls and house corners are what the farmers left. Many soldiers in both wings are their great-grandchildren.
- **The Seam Survey.** Host surveyors mapped Vesper's twin seams and reported that they all run back toward one origin. Their conclusion: the Starforge is on Vesper.
- **The split Muster.** Asked for its counsel, the Muster divided evenly, and the division fell along the wings.
  - **Blue Wing, the Kindlers.** Find it and relight it. The purposed things are failing one by one, a people has a right to its inheritance, and to work the light again is the fullest communion there is.
  - **Red Wing, the Keepers.** Find it and seal it cold under both seals. The fire went home, and taking it down a second time is theft. Whoever holds a lit Starforge needs neither Khan nor Mother.
  - Each suspects the worst: Red believes Blue's captains want the fire for themselves; Blue believes Red means to break the Starforge and call it piety.
- **The Silence.** Either leader could end the war with a sentence. Neither has spoken. The common explanations:
  - Each waits for the other, because one hand ruling alone on a matter of both breaks the Compact.
  - The Khan owes his seat to the Matriarch and will not speak before she does; she will not speak because any ruling defines doctrine on the fire for ever.
  - They agreed on silence together and are letting the wings exhaust themselves.
  - They disagree with each other, and the war below is theirs by proxy.
- **In law.** No wing has been named rebel. Both are lawful hosts of the Empire, and officially there is no civil war, only "a disputed custody".

## 6. The war on Vesper
- **Shape.** No front line. Dozens of small engagements, each in a fresh area of operations along a seam, each wing following the ore toward an origin neither has reached.
- **The irony everyone has noticed.** The seams are the only map to the Starforge, and both wings dig them up to print tanks. The war eats its own map.
- **The Commander.** A field officer of a wing with a service record like any other. Deliberately blank. In the game as it stands the posting is to the Blue Wing and the officer across the field is Red; the game's text takes no side in the wings' quarrel, so the posting can become a choice later.
- **The enemy who knows you.** Before the split, the Host's **Annalists** kept a dossier on every officer. Each wing took half the archive and all of the habit. The officer across the field (graded Recruit, Veteran or Commander) is served by a staff that studied you in peacetime and amends the file after every engagement. It sees only what its own units see, because the orders in orbit share nothing with either staff.
- **The oath.** Every soldier swears the Host-oath. Fear breaks some of them, and the broken go to ground behind their own base. Aircrews do not: there is nowhere to set down among the broken.
- **Capes.** A soldier who proves themselves takes a second, stricter oath before a knight and receives the knight's cape in the wing's colour, with the Holy Mother's palmette in the lower corners, sprinkled with star-water at vespers. Its trim is bronze, and gold for those who prove themselves again. No soldier in a cape is known to have run.
- **Diggers.** Guild labour, sworn to the Foundry and not to the war. They flee guns, never the guild.
- **The Biolab.** The breeders' craft is a science of the Long Night with no starfire in it. Its stock is what the Clearance left on Vesper: feral cattle, reed-bed frogs, wasps, spiders. The Luminary permits it on a narrow argument: a bred creature swears nothing, so it can break nothing. Soldiers put it more simply: they never desert. Most knights call it a purpose forced on a living thing, which is the one thing the Makers never did.

## 7. The knightly orders
- **Origin.** Older than the Empire and older than the Luminary. They descend from the guards of the Makers and hold what remains of the Makers' greatest work. They keep the hours by their own, older use.
- **Cores.** At the heart of every Mech sits a **core**, a purposed mind forged in the Bright Age. No one can make another. Frames, legs and weapons are modern and interchangeable; the core is the Mech. Since the Quenching a core can think, remember and advise, but cannot move a limb without a sworn human hand.
- **Two voices.** By the orders' teaching, the calls, reports and verdicts are the knight's, and the quiet quoted sayings are the core's ("The enemy has experience. I have memory."). A core takes on something of the temper of the hand it is sworn to. Sceptics hold there is no second voice, only knights reciting their order's book of sayings. Knights who are asked say "we" and change the subject.
- **The High Watch.** The orders keep ships over any world where the Host fights. Orbit is neutral and has been since before the Compact.
- **The Gantry Right.** The orders swear to neither Khan nor Mother nor wing. They are bound to answer: any lawful host that raises a gantry is owed one knight and one core, once, for that field. Because the Silence leaves both wings lawful, the orders must answer both. If either leader named a wing rebel, its gantries would go unanswered. Both wings know it.
- **One per war.** A knight counts a war from the lit gantry to the decided field. One core is all an order will stake on it. A fallen Mech is not replaced, and a fallen gantry cancels the summons.
- **Fitting-out.** The Vigilants of the High Watch see the whole field and fit the Mech in the last seconds before the drop, to answer what the enemy has brought. By agreement among the orders, the two Mechs on a field are seldom fitted alike, so no knight can claim the other was favoured.
- **Gifts.** A host may give its knight better servos, armour or an uplink. A knight accepts gifts and asks for none.
- **After the fall.** The order's serjeants walk the field afterwards to bring the core home. Not every recovered core wakes again. This war is spending cores faster than any in the record, which each wing cites as proof of its own case.
- **A halved oath.** When knights of one order answer both gantries, they fight in earnest. The survivor carries the fallen standard home, and no grudge is permitted.
- **The verdict.** Knights are the keepers of oaths, and custom gives them judgement over the oathbroken. Whether a knight hunts deserters or spares them is the knight's own conscience. The orders do not rule on it.
- **The licensed lance.** Only an order may sanction a weapon built solely to kill a knight. So each host's single Mech-Hunter rides under an order's flag, crewed by its serjeants, one lance at a time. The orders' reasoning: a knight who cannot be killed is a tyrant.
- **The Ironclad** is the Host's own imitation: a small walker with no core and nothing to say.

Reputation is hearsay. In the field a knight's own temper and pride decide, not the order.

**Titles are offices, not ranks.** A Castellan keeps an order's hold, a Seneschal its household, a Marshal its knights in the field, a Knight-Warden a single core. A Paladin is a sworn champion, a Knight-Errant answers the gantries nobody else will, and a Vigilant has served on the High Watch.

## 8. The Starforge
- **What is said.** A hearth no larger than a village smithy, with a fire that needed no fuel. Other accounts say an anvil, a crucible, a hammer, or a place and not a thing. No description is older than the Empire.
- **What each party fears.** The Kindlers, that it will be found and wasted. The Keepers, that it will be found and used. The Luminary and the Camp, that it will be found by one hand. The orders have not said.
- **What a few surveyors suspect.** Twin seams from one pour, all running back to one origin: the teal may not point to the Starforge. It may *be* the Starforge, broken at the Quenching and scattered as slag. If so there is nothing to find, and both wings are feeding it into their foundries by the tonne.
- **What the Ninth Vigil knows.** Unstated. They were there.
- **The name.** Soldiers of both wings call the whole campaign "the Starforge", as in "I did two seasons in the Starforge".

## 9. Soldiers' lore: the Reckoning
A Vesper folk-tale carried by the Clearance families: the beasts of the fallow land keep the oldest oath of all, and a commander who wins by foul means does not keep the win. The soldiers run, and the animals have the last word. The Luminary neither teaches nor denies it. No officer admits to believing it.

## 13. Writing rules
For anything the game says or shows. Read this section before writing player-facing text, a name, a line of speech or an art prompt.

**Who speaks, and how**
- **The knight** (the Mech's tagged calls, its arrival, its verdicts on deserters): plain and present, with places and numbers. Says "I", addresses "Commander".
- **The core** (the Mech's quiet quoted lines): old, certain, restrained and dry. One or two short sentences, sometimes three. Never rants, begs or jokes broadly. British spelling. It may remember the Bright Age; it declines to explain it.
- **Soldiers** (squad reports, alerts): who, where, what. No lore.
- **The game's own voice** (menus, manual, tips): plain modern English. Where it explains how the opponent works it may say "the AI"; headings and flavour use the world's names.
- **The Annalist** (the intro's narrator): a dry archivist briefing an officer. Never says which wing the listener serves.

**Never answered.** No text confirms or denies anything in section 11. A speaker may refuse to say; nobody may say.

**No side taken.** Nothing the game says in its own voice, or the knight's, or the core's, prefers the Kindlers or the Keepers. The orders serve the field, not the quarrel.

**The enemy is the other wing of one Host.** Lawful, under the same oath. Never "rebels", "traitors", "heretics" or "aliens". A speaker may hold their skill in contempt; nobody hates them.

**Words**
- Use: starfire, the Makers, purpose and purposed, the Quenching, the Long Night, the Brightest Star, the Host, wing, Kindlers, Keepers, the Compact, the Silence, the Muster, the Luminary, lauds and vespers, star-water, seam, printworks, the Clearance, gantry, core, knight, order, the High Watch, Vigilants, the Gantry Right, serjeants, Annalists, dossier or file.
- The ore has three names by speaker: **quenchstone** (formal), **teal** (soldiers), **ore** (the game's interface).
- The Mech's rider is a **knight** in the world and may be the **pilot** in the interface.
- Avoid: magic, spell, sorcery (except to deny it), gods, prayers that ask for something (the light is received, not petitioned), Earth's places and dates, "robot" or "program" for a core.

**Numbers that must agree**
- The fire went out 1,000 years ago; 300 years of house wars; the Empire is 700 years old; the Clearance was 100 years ago.
- A Mech stands seven to nine metres. One knight and one core a side, a field.
